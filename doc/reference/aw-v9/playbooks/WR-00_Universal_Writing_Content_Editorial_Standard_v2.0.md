## Executive standard

**Writing is a decision about what a particular reader needs to understand, believe, remember, decide or do—and the least distorting way to make that possible.** It is not a contest to produce the most elegant sentence, fewest words, most headings, most persuasive rhythm, or most extensive edit.

The governing chain is:

```text
READER / AFFECTED PARTIES
→ REAL NEED + READING / USE CONTEXT
→ COMMUNICATION PURPOSE + REQUIRED OUTCOME
→ CONTENT SELECTION + EVIDENCE
→ INFORMATION HIERARCHY
→ APPROPRIATE STRUCTURE / FORMAT
→ PRECISE WORDING + TERMINOLOGY
→ VOICE / TONE / LOCALIZATION
→ DIAGNOSIS + EDITING
→ CLAIM / MEANING / ACCESSIBILITY VERIFICATION
→ READER VALIDATION WHEN CONSEQUENCE WARRANTS
→ LEARNING + CONTROLLED EVOLUTION
```

The chain is a **reasoning model, not a compulsory linear drafting pipeline**. Good writing frequently iterates between the intended reader, what is known, the claim, and the chosen form.

The governing doctrine:

> **Diagnose before rewriting. Preserve before improving. Every material change must solve an identifiable problem. Keep what is accurate, purposeful, understandable and distinctive; change only what improves the reader's outcome without losing meaning, evidence, context, or authorial intent.**

This playbook makes **three asymmetric requirements**:

1. **Reader success outranks text polish.** A beautiful rewrite that changes what the reader understands is a regression.
2. **The existing draft has preservation value.** Editing has a burden of justification; `KEEP` is a valid—and often best—decision.
3. **Verification outranks verbal confidence.** Accurate-sounding prose, source-like references, and polished AI output do not establish truth.

Its plain-language quality anchor is **relevance, findability, understandability and usability**, consistent with ISO 24495-1:2023's published scope and public formulation [ISO01]. Writing quality remains wider: some works also legitimately optimize argument, voice, originality, nuance, emotion, scholarly precision or artistic effect. Those contextual aims must never be erased by applying public-service heuristics as universal laws.

### One-page operating rule

```text
IF the reader, purpose, material facts or authorial constraints are unknown:
    Establish them before a consequential rewrite; otherwise mark uncertainty.
IF existing text serves its purpose and has no material defect:
    KEEP; do not cosmetically rewrite it.
ELSE:
    State the defect and its consequence.
    Select the smallest intervention that can fix it.
    Preserve locked facts, intent, voice and necessary nuance.
    Compare candidate against original, not against generic AI fluency.
    Verify changed meaning, claims, conditions, numbers and citations.
    Test with readers when stakes or uncertainty justify it.
    Accept only a demonstrable net improvement; otherwise revert.
```

## V2 executive research and correction verdict — 10 October 2026

**What changed:** V1 already established the correct core: reader need before style; content before polish; source verification; `KEEP` as a legitimate editorial result. V2 preserves those invariants **rather than rewriting working doctrine**. It expands primary-source and empirical coverage, strengthens falsification, resolves hidden scope/authority traps, and supplies stronger execution/verification instruments.

**Eight material V2 corrections:**

1. **Editing restraint gains direct professional corroboration.** The Chartered Institute of Editing and Proofreading (CIEP) explicitly treats author-voice preservation, restraint, suitable depth of intervention and the agreed brief as professional editorial duties. *The Chicago Manual of Style* distinguishes line and copyediting; Harvard/Purdue prioritize substantive structure before cosmetic revision. These are **institutional/practitioner standards and teaching methods**, not randomized proof that minimal edits always outperform a necessary major rewrite [CIEP26][CIEP-GLOSS][CHI18][HAR01][PUR01][PUR02].
2. **The AI homogenization risk is better supported and more carefully scoped.** A peer-reviewed August 2026 meta-analysis of 19 empirical studies (61 effects) found a small pooled convergence effect with substantial task-dependent variation. That finding concerns **semantic/idea diversity in human–AI co-creation**, not a quantified probability of individual voice destruction during copyediting [AIMETA26].
3. **Jargon has a credibility–comprehension trap.** Nine 2025 experiments found that scientific jargon could increase lay readers' satisfaction with explanations while decreasing comprehension. Another 2025 study did *not* replicate certain feared downsides of jargon avoidance. Therefore: do not optimize for how authoritative the text sounds, and do not mechanically delete legitimate domain terms [JARG25A][JARG25B].
4. **Plain language is not a complete quality score.** AHRQ separates comprehensibility and actionability and explicitly says its PEMAT tool cannot certify factual accuracy or guarantee effect for the target patient group. This supports separating clarity, truth, usable action and observed outcome [AHRQPEMAT].
5. **Institutional style guides are implementation evidence, not universal experiments.** Google explicitly allows deviation when readers benefit. Apple (June 2026), Microsoft, GOV.UK and the European Commission share context-sensitive clarity themes yet differ in purpose, tone, and local house conventions. Do not standardize every medium into public-service microcopy [GOOG26][APP26][MIC01][EU01].
6. **Editorial sourcing is an independent gate.** Reuters' journalistic standards prioritize truthful sourcing, exact attribution, correction and fact/opinion boundaries; January 2026 ICMJE recommendations assign human accountability for AI-assisted content and require publisher-specific disclosure. These obligations are scoped to the respective institutions; WR-00 adopts a proportional integrity control, not their entire publishing regime [REUTERS][ICMJE26].
7. **Accessibility has separate normative layers.** WCAG 2.2 conformance requirements, W3C's *informative* COGA writing advice and actual reader testing are complementary rather than synonyms. WCAG's reading-level provision is Level AAA, and permits supplemental content instead of a global vocabulary ban [ACC01][ACC03][COGA].
8. **The strongest available outcome is reader performance, not a polished paragraph.** The relevant evidence distinguishes findability, accurate comprehension, correct application, persuasive/creative purpose, verification burden and original voice. V2 therefore adds **evaluation by genre, authority and failure severity**, not one overall writing number [AHRQPEMAT][PLS01][HAR01][MPS].

**Falsification bottom line:** The V1 doctrine survived; no credible evidence justifies mandatory full rewrites, one universal word limit, one optimal structure, or trusting fluent AI output without checking content. Conversely, `KEEP` is **not** a prohibition on legitimate artistic transformation, authorized repositioning, accessibility remediation or needed structural change. For material edits, the operative condition is a named defect **or** a documented change of purpose/constraints. The audit distinguishes what *has* been independently empirically tested from a `HOUSE` risk-control judgment. This release remains `REVIEWED`, not `TESTED` or `VALIDATED`.

# 1. Purpose, uses, and deliberate boundaries

## 1.1 What WR-00 owns

WR-00 owns **universal editorial quality and controlled text transformation** across genres and channels: audience/need clarification, content selection, hierarchy, structure, linguistic clarity, coherence, terminology, voice preservation, source fidelity, editing judgment, factual qualification, accessibility at the language/semantic layer, localization review, and editorial verification.

A conforming human or AI assistant can explain **why a piece of writing exists; what information its reader needs; why each substantial element remains; what any change is intended to improve; and how meaning and evidence were protected.**

It does **not** own everything communicated through words. A channel, service, brand or creative project can require a specialist objective and legitimate exceptions to WR-00 defaults.

## 1.2 Interfaces; specialist ownership is not duplicated

| Adjacent playbook | It owns | WR-00 owns | Input → output contract |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Brand Strategy & Positioning** | Category/frame, target buying situations, positioning, strategic value proposition, distinctive verbal assets, approved proof/message pillars and brand codes [BRAND]. | Fidelity and clarity of text expressing those decisions; protects approved terminology and distinctive voice. | Brand passes approved message/proof/voice constraints → WR-00 returns reader-fit text and a fidelity check; strategic repositioning routes back to Brand. |
| **UX** | End-to-end task, mental model, flow, friction, interaction/state, navigation, error recovery, usability testing [UX]. | Clarity and accessibility of sentences, labels, instructions, error wording and explanation as language. | UX defines state, action, consequences and placement → WR-00 edits expression; if wording cannot repair a confusing experience, route back to UX, not more copy. |
| **Organic Discoverability** | Search/answer intent, topic and keyword research, entities, crawl/index/AI eligibility, content portfolio, links, distribution and organic measurement [ORGANIC]. | Accuracy, useful content, structure, claims, coherence, editorial voice; no search-term stuffing. | Organic defines retrieval need, subject and discovery constraints → WR-00 produces useful verified prose → Organic checks eligibility and reach. |
| **Copywriting** (specialist interface; not supplied here) | Persuasion, offer framing, purchase objections, CTA strategy, conversion experiments, channel creative and customer decision psychology. | Editorial integrity, precision, honesty, clarity, voice and change control of resulting copy. | Copywriting defines commercial job and persuasive hypothesis → WR-00 checks expression; WR-00 cannot invent benefits/proof or replace conversion strategy. |
| **Storytelling** (specialist interface; not supplied here) | Narrative premise, plot, stakes, character, point of view, tension, scene, pacing and thematic effect. | Sentence/paragraph coherence, factual integrity where applicable, intended voice, structural clarity where desired, controlled editing. | Storytelling defines intended emotional/narrative arc → WR-00 preserves it; deliberate ambiguity and repetition may be narrative assets. |

**Collision rule.** Applicable law and accessible/accurate communication floors prevail over stylistic preferences. Within those constraints the specialist owner decides the **communication job**, and WR-00 decides whether the words faithfully and effectively execute it. A real conflict is escalated and decided explicitly; neither playbook may silently overwrite another's core decision. The Brand, UX and Organic scope allocations are taken from the supplied project files; the Copywriting and Storytelling contracts are proposed `HOUSE` interfaces, **not claims about missing source playbooks**.

## 1.3 Use at three execution levels

- **Low-risk everyday communication (L1–L2):** identify the reader and purpose; use the keep/edit decision and compact gates. No change ledger needed for an obvious typo.
- **Material public, customer, professional or organizational text (L3):** source/intent basis, diagnosis, material-diff rationale, claims and voice checks, suitable reviewer and reader task test.
- **High-consequence legal, financial, medical, safety, regulated or irrevocable communication (L4):** domain-specialist authority, traceability for critical qualifiers, independent review, locale/accessibility checks and versioned approval; WR-00 is **not** the domain expert.

Risk is a function of **consequence, ambiguity, audience vulnerability, scale, irreversibility, uncertainty, source weakness and ease of detecting/correcting an error**—not text length or channel prestige [MPS].

## 1.4 Types of writing; no single template

| Communication job | First question | Often fitting organization | Do not impose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Task/instruction | What must the person do correctly? | prerequisite → step/action → result/error/recovery | Essay introduction or promotional hook |
| Decision brief | What decision and evidence are needed? | recommendation/decision → grounds → options/trade-offs → uncertainties | Chronology for its own sake |
| Factual explanation | What does the reader need to understand? | answer/definition → mechanism → concrete example → limitations | Forced conversion CTA |
| Research/report | What did the method show and not show? | question → method → results → interpretation/limits, per domain rules | Claim stronger than study permits |
| Status/update | What changed, matters and happens next? | outcome/change → consequence → owner/next action | Full backstory |
| Relationship message | What does this recipient need to know and feel respected by? | context → specific message → response/action when apt | Formulaic headings and bullets |
| Editorial/argument | What viewpoint, support and counterargument matter? | thesis or hook → evidence/ideas → tension → conclusion | Mechanical inverted pyramid in every case |
| Fiction/story | What experience is intended? | defined by Storytelling / artistic intent | Plain-language reduction of purposeful imagery or ambiguity |

A communication may serve more than one purpose, but the primary objective and its trade-offs must be visible.

# 2. Evidence, research protocol, and epistemic hygiene

## 2.1 Inheritance from MPS-001

This specialist standard follows the supplied **Master Playbook Standard v2.0-RC1** for outcome-first scoping, source-fit evidence, R1–R4 rigor, claim classification, contradiction handling, independence, four-layer playbook architecture, atomic Plays, quality gates, scenario validation, traceability and status [MPS]. It specializes MPS §19 for broader writing; it does not replace or weaken that governing section.

The evidence path is:

```text
QUESTION + CONSEQUENCE
→ FIT-FOR-QUESTION SOURCE(S)
→ STUDY / AUTHORITY / PRACTICE STATUS
→ CONTRADICTORY OR LIMITING EVIDENCE
→ CLAIM SCOPE + CONFIDENCE
→ EDITORIAL RULE (NOT AUTOMATICALLY A MUST)
→ EXECUTABLE DECISION / TEST
→ OBSERVED READER OUTCOME
```

Do **not** turn a source's prestige into evidence, a formal standard into a causal trial, a readability score into comprehension, or a single experiment into a universal editing rule.

## 2.2 Claim and requirement vocabulary

- `REQ`: externally binding in the **named scope**; cites the authority and application condition.
- `EST`: well-established mechanism supported by relevant independent evidence streams; requires contextual limits.
- `DEF`: reasonable default, not compulsory in every genre.
- `CTX`: situational practice with explicit boundary conditions.
- `EMG`: emerging and subject to re-verification.
- `HOUSE`: deliberate WR-00 governance/control choice; cannot be misrepresented as empirical law.
- `EXP`: testable hypothesis with a decision rule.
- `UNK`: unresolved material uncertainty.

`MUST` and `MUST NOT` are required **inside WR-00** unless explicitly described as `REQ` from another authority. `SHOULD` permits reasoned exceptions. `MAY` is optional. `JUDGMENT REQUIRED` blocks false determinism. **Normative force and empirical confidence are distinct** [MPS].

Evidence lanes for this domain:

| Evidence family | Appropriate claim | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| Binding law/contract/editorial policy | What must be included/disclosed in that scope | Mistaking obligation for global writing optimization |
| Formal standard (ISO/WCAG, etc.) | Normative baseline and defined accessibility/plain-language dimensions | Mistaking standard consensus for measured performance uplift |
| Systematic review/meta-analysis | Aggregated intervention patterns and heterogeneity | Pretending populations/genres are interchangeable |
| Controlled reading/writing/HCI studies | Effect of a specific intervention on a specific group/task | Universalizing a lab result or old tool version |
| Government/open institutional guidance | Actionable applied default or control | Treating another agency's house limit as a law of reading |
| Practitioner/editorial guide | Formatting and editing hypothesis | Popularity, aesthetic preference and commercial bias |
| Reader/organization evidence | Actual clarity, task success, support load, credibility and style fit | Inferring causal benefit from self-reported preference alone |
| AI-generated evaluation | Diagnostic suggestions, possible inconsistency | Circular grading or invented sources as independent proof |

Confidence for a claim: `HIGH`, `MODERATE`, `LOW`, `INSUFFICIENT`. No overall “100% scientifically proven WR-00” rating is permitted.

## 2.4 Current formal-source baseline at 2026-10-10

- **ISO 24495-1:2023** is published and supplies the universal plain-language foundation [ISO01].
- **ISO 24495-2:2025** is published and specializes legal communication [ISO02].
- **ISO 24495-3:2026** is published (May 2026), specializing **plain science writing for varied audiences**, *not* the same thing as specialist-to-specialist scientific journal reporting [ISO03].
- **ISO/DIS 24495-4** on organization-level implementation remains a **Draft International Standard / under development**, not the current final baseline [ISO04].
- **WCAG 2.2** is the published web accessibility standard. Requirements differ by conformance level; informative W3C cognitive-accessibility patterns are valuable guidance but do **not** automatically become WCAG requirements [ACC01][ACC02].
- Platform/style-guide sentence/paragraph targets (for example GOV.UK guidance) are **local defaults**, not universal psychological thresholds [GOV02].

## 2.5 The evidence-based core and its important limits

1. The **reader, need and task** are strong organizing criteria in public-service content and plain-language guidance [ISO01][GOV01][CDC01]. This is an applied/normative baseline, not a claim that every poem needs an action button.
2. **Structural cues** can improve finding, remembering or understanding selected information; experimental effects vary with cue quality, reader expertise and task [COG01][COG02]. They do not justify gratuitous headings on a three-sentence message.
3. Simplification often improves perceived readability/comprehension in patient-information interventions, yet a 2025 review found **mixed evidence for behavioral or health outcomes** and generally low study quality [HL01]. Do not equate easy reading with correct action.
4. **Reader prior knowledge matters.** Classic coherence experiments showed that explicit coherence helped some readers, while demanding inferencing sometimes benefited high-knowledge readers on particular learning measures; some results failed to replicate across tasks [COG03]. “Most explanation possible” is not universal.
5. **Readability formulas** are mechanical proxies; AHRQ warns they do not measure actual comprehension and can disagree [READ01]. Use them as diagnostic outlier signals, not release certification.
6. **AI writing support has real benefits in bounded tasks**: a preregistered 2023 experiment reported reduced time and improved ratings on professional writing tasks [AI01]. That finding does not guarantee quality in safety-critical, creative or current-model use.
7. **AI assistance can narrow variety or shift style/positions** in particular experiments [AI02][AI03][AI04]. This supports protecting author intent and treating model prose as suggestions—not a ban on AI.
8. **AI simplification can omit or alter crucial information.** Studies show both beneficial readability and content-fidelity failures; reliable evaluation compares output with the original and qualified sources, not only a surface readability metric [AI05][AI06][AI07].
9. **Reference integrity is a distinct control.** ICMJE places responsibility for accurate, claim-supporting references on authors; NIST's GenAI risk profile identifies confidently wrong content and invented citations as risks [SRC01][AI08].
10. **Popular plain-language criteria are not all experimentally validated.** A systematic review of 90 papers, 33 of which empirically tested relevant effects, found insufficient consistent evidence for most criteria in existing plain-language summary guidelines. WR-00 must remain specific about what is `HOUSE` versus empirically established [PLS01].

11. **2026 meta-analytic AI evidence:** pooled cross-output convergence is small and varies materially with task; it does not show an automatic quality loss for the individual writer [AIMETA26].
12. **Scientific jargon can create misplaced satisfaction:** explaining a mechanism well and *sounding expert* are separable outcomes; a satisfied reader may still be mistaken [JARG25A]. A second experiment provides counterevidence to presumed harms of jargon avoidance [JARG25B].
13. **Text signaling is supported, but effect transfer is bounded:** a 2018 meta-analysis of educational media reports average learning advantages, but that is not direct evidence that every heading on every website creates the same benefit [COG02]. A direct 2011 experimental text study finds different heading organizations support different retrieval/outlining tasks [COG01].
14. **Product editorial guidance explicitly values context:** Apple’s June 2026 Style Guide and design guidance distinguish stable voice from situational tone; Google's July 2026 style updates and guide explicitly allow domain-justified exceptions [APP26][GOOG26].
15. **Trusted publication requires more than correct-looking citations:** Reuters requires sound sourcing and transparent correction; ICMJE’s January 2026 guidance requires humans to verify AI-authored material and follow venue-specific disclosure rules [REUTERS][ICMJE26].
16. **No single experimental metric settles writing quality.** Even the patient-focused AHRQ PEMAT separates understandability from actionability and does not measure factual accuracy; use task- and risk-specific evidence [AHRQPEMAT].

# 3. Quality model and protected invariants

A text can be concise yet wrong, exhaustive yet unusable, polished yet bland, accessible yet misleading, or distinctive yet incomprehensible. Evaluate dimensions separately.

| Dimension | Evaluation question | Prohibited proxy |
|---|---|---|
| **Relevance** | Does every important element serve this reader and purpose? | Topic coverage count |
| **Findability / hierarchy** | Can readers locate the answer, decision, caveat or step when needed? | Number of headings |
| **Comprehensibility** | Can intended readers form a correct mental model? | Reading-grade score alone |
| **Accuracy / integrity** | Are the facts, scope, quotes, conditions and uncertainty preserved? | Fluent confidence |
| **Precision** | Can a material assertion be interpreted as intended without harmful ambiguity? | More technical words |
| **Concision / economy** | Is anything unnecessary, while necessary qualifiers remain? | Lowest word count |
| **Specificity / usefulness** | Are needed actors, objects, steps, examples or evidence concrete? | More detail irrespective of need |
| **Coherence** | Are propositions connected and ordered logically? | Mechanical transition words |
| **Voice / authorship** | Does the work still sound and mean like its intended author/brand? | Generic professional tone |
| **Accessibility / inclusion** | Can relevant audiences perceive, navigate and understand the material? | Automated checker pass |
| **Localization** | Is the meaning usable in target language/culture/locale? | Literal translation accuracy |
| **Credibility / provenance** | Can consequential claims be traced to appropriate evidence? | Long citation list |
| **Fit for action / learning** | Does the writing actually enable the intended decision/task/experience? | Writer/LLM preference alone |

**Protected invariants (`HOUSE`; mandatory during editing):**

- `MEANING`: no altered material proposition, causal relation, negation, threshold, uncertainty or scope without authorization.
- `FACTS`: no invented dates, numbers, people, source claims, quotations, results, capabilities or promises.
- `INTENT`: do not change what the speaker is trying to say, ask, emphasize, concede, or leave unsaid.
- `VOICE`: do not flatten valued rhythm, cadence, diction, perspective, humor, register or distinctive phrases merely because they are unconventional.
- `READER`: do not shift assumptions about audience knowledge, decision role, language, vulnerability or task without deciding to do so.
- `EVIDENCE`: preserve distinctions among observed fact, inference, opinion, hypothesis, example and unknown.
- `CONDITIONS`: preserve legal/safety/financial qualifiers, exclusions and procedural sequence.
- `FORMAT`: preserve requested output form, important accessibility structure and the genre's necessary conventions.
- `AUTHORSHIP`: an editor may propose; material decisions remain with the accountable author/owner.

If a change cannot protect an invariant, make the conflict visible and obtain the appropriate authorization rather than silently trading it away.

---

### V2 — Four irreducible assurance questions

A well-edited piece should be assessed with four separate questions, never compressed into a decorative writing score:

| Question | Claim verified | Evidence that fits | Failure if skipped |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Is it right?** | Factual claims, logic, attributed views, quotations, omissions and certainty | Primary sources, specialist review, source-to-claim matrix | Fluent misinformation, altered legal/technical meaning |
| **Can this reader use it?** | The intended reader can locate, understand, decide or act correctly | Representative task tests, comprehension probes, realistic user context | Attractive text without useful comprehension |
| **Is it the intended text?** | Genre, message, tone, personality and authorial intent survived the transformation | Author/brand comparison, protected-spans check, legitimate stakeholder acceptance | Generic AI prose, message drift, culturally flattened voice |
| **Was the edit worth doing?** | Expected benefit exceeds verification/rework, regression and opportunity cost | Identifiable defect, minimal diff, option comparison; pilot data for scaled automation | Unnecessary full rewrites, bigger process burden than gain |

For high-risk publication, **any one failed critical invariant is a blocker**, even if other dimensions score well. The primary outcome differs by genre: exact execution for instructions, calibrated understanding for research, useful decisions for memos, accurate human connection for messages, narrative effect for literature and authorized commercial communication for persuasion. Do not confuse *a writer preferring a draft* with *a reader understanding it correctly* [MPS][AHRQPEMAT][PLS01].

# 4. The Golden Writing Standards

Each rule is tagged with **claim type / confidence**; source references refer to §16. `HIGH` describes justified belief in the scoped mechanism, not a guarantee in every use case. Grouping is for navigation, not a request to apply every rule equally to every sentence.

## 4.1 Reader, need and purpose

1. **WR-01 — Define the reader before drafting a material communication.** Distinguish actual reader, approver, affected party, decision maker, expertise and access constraints when they differ. `EST/HIGH` [ISO01][GOV01].
2. **WR-02 — Define the reader's situation, not an abstract persona.** Capture why/when the text is encountered, prior knowledge, urgency, distractions, stakes and what the reader already knows. `DEF/HIGH` [GOV01][ACC02].
3. **WR-03 — Name the communication job.** Inform, instruct, help decide, persuade, document, coordinate, entertain, explore or maintain a relationship; identify the main outcome. `HOUSE/HIGH` [MPS][CDC01].
4. **WR-04 — Distinguish reader need from author desire.** “We want to announce a launch” is not yet “Customers need to know what changed and whether to act.” `DEF/HIGH` [GOV01].
5. **WR-05 — Decide whether a new text is needed.** No content is better than redundant, misleading or unsupported content. Update, link, merge or decline when the need is already served. `HOUSE/MODERATE` [MPS][ORGANIC].
6. **WR-06 — Include stakeholders who can be harmed by misunderstanding, not just the immediate recipient.** More rigor for consequential communication. `DEF/HIGH` [MPS][SRC01].
7. **WR-07 — Do not force a call to action where action is not the purpose.** An explanation, apology, poem or literary passage can legitimately end without a command. `CTX/HIGH` [CDC01][GOV01].
8. **WR-08 — Prefer reader evidence to imaginary certainty.** Use support inquiries, search terms, usability observations, interviews or documented tasks where possible. `DEF/HIGH` [CDC01][GOV01].

## 4.2 Content selection, truth and information hierarchy

9. **WR-09 — Choose the needed content before polishing sentences.** Select propositions and evidence by contribution to the reader's outcome. `EST/HIGH` [ISO01][CDC01].
10. **WR-10 — Separate must-know, useful-detail and optional background.** Do not let the background crowd out the answer. `DEF/HIGH` [GOV01][CDC01].
11. **WR-11 — Give each material claim a knowable status.** Fact, documented report, inference, opinion, estimate, example, recommendation or unknown. `HOUSE/HIGH` [MPS][SRC01].
12. **WR-12 — Retain necessary qualifications.** A concise sentence that loses a risk condition, base rate, denominator, exception or uncertainty is worse than a longer accurate one. `HOUSE/HIGH` [READ01][AI05].
13. **WR-13 — Put the important thing where the reader can find it.** Frontload an answer in task/decision content when useful; do not force suspense-based writing into a report. `DEF/HIGH` [GOV01][CDC01].
14. **WR-14 — Structure for retrieval and understanding, not for appearance.** Headings, links, summaries, tables and lists must help navigation, comparison, comprehension or execution. `EST/HIGH` [COG01][COG02][ACC01].
15. **WR-15 — Organize related ideas together.** Show which facts explain, qualify, contrast with or follow from others. `EST/HIGH` [COG02][COG03].
16. **WR-16 — Choose an order based on the task.** Chronology, causal sequence, comparison, priority, problem→response, research method or narrative arc are contextual options. `CTX/HIGH` [MPS][GOV01].
17. **WR-17 — Do not add content solely to make the piece look complete.** A three-sentence answer can be excellent; a technical argument can need 30 pages. `HOUSE/HIGH` [READ01][GOV02].
18. **WR-18 — Give critical caveats equal findability.** Do not hide limitations in a footnote if they change the main decision. `DEF/HIGH` [SRC01][ACC02].

## 4.3 Wording, terminology, sentence and paragraph craft

19. **WR-19 — Prefer familiar, concrete, exact language when it preserves the meaning.** “What will change?” beats vague abstractions; precision outranks shortness. `EST/HIGH` [GOV02][ACC02].
20. **WR-20 — State actors, actions, objects, conditions and times explicitly when their absence creates risk.** Avoid ambiguous pronouns or unspecified responsibility. `DEF/HIGH` [GOV02].
21. **WR-21 — Use active voice by default where it clarifies agency.** Passive voice is legitimate when result/recipient matters more, the actor is unknown, or disclosure rules favor it. `CTX/HIGH` [GOV02].
22. **WR-22 — Avoid unnecessary nominalization and abstract verbs.** Replace “perform an evaluation of” with “evaluate” when no distinction is lost; retain nominal forms that name a real domain object or necessary technical distinction. `DEF/HIGH` [GOV02].
23. **WR-23 — Keep one coherent idea per sentence *unless* a relationship between ideas is clearer together.** Do not chop causal/conditional logic into isolated fragments. `DEF/MODERATE` [GOV02][COG03].
24. **WR-24 — Treat sentence and paragraph length as diagnostic, not magic.** Shorten when complexity impairs understanding; retain longer units when rhythm or linked reasoning requires them. `CTX/HIGH` [GOV02][READ01].
25. **WR-25 — Write paragraphs around one meaningful unit of thought, with visible progression.** Paragraph breaks aid navigation; ultra-short fragments can destroy the flow of prose. `DEF/HIGH` [GOV02][COG02].
26. **WR-26 — Use domain terms when they communicate necessary distinctions.** Define them at the reader's first need; do not replace `authentication` with `logging in` when security distinctions matter. `CTX/HIGH` [GOV02][ISO03].
27. **WR-27 — One concept should normally have one stable term.** Do not alternate “customer,” “account,” “user” and “member” if they identify different roles. `DEF/HIGH` [ONS01].
28. **WR-28 — Resolve references and scope.** Specify what “it,” “this,” “they,” “some,” “often,” “best,” and “significant” mean when ambiguity changes a conclusion. `DEF/HIGH` [SRC01].
29. **WR-29 — Prefer meaningful specificity over decorative detail.** An actor, mechanism, example, date or measurable consequence is useful when needed; gratuitous numbers are not. `DEF/HIGH` [CDC01].
30. **WR-30 — Use numbers and comparisons intelligibly.** Preserve base, period, population, units, absolute vs relative risk and uncertainty. `HOUSE/HIGH` [CDC01][SRC01].
31. **WR-31 — State uncertainty at the scope where it belongs.** Neither hide uncertainty in false certainty nor weaken every sentence with generic hedging. `HOUSE/HIGH` [SRC01][AI08].
32. **WR-32 — Use repetition when it performs a job.** Reinforce an essential instruction, aid retention, maintain terminology, make a deliberate rhetorical pattern or orient after a section; delete accidental restatement. `CTX/MODERATE` [COG01][COG03].

## 4.4 Voice, tone, originality and accessibility

33. **WR-33 — Define voice as stable characteristics; tone as situational expression.** Voice can include syntax, vocabulary, rhythm, perspective and degree of informality. Tone adapts to stakes, reader and channel. `HOUSE/HIGH` [BRAND][MS01].
34. **WR-34 — Preserve the author's distinctive voice unless a named defect makes change necessary.** Eccentric is not automatically unclear; polish is not automatically progress. `HOUSE/HIGH` [AI02][AI03].
35. **WR-35 — Keep the degree of certainty and emotional intensity proportionate.** Avoid hype, forced empathy, false intimacy, theatrical profundity and generic cheerleading. `DEF/MODERATE` [GOV02][AI04].
36. **WR-36 — Do not mistake neutrality for quality.** Clear writing can be playful, skeptical, direct, poetic, warm, urgent or restrained when context calls for it. `CTX/HIGH` [MS01][AI02].
37. **WR-37 — Make language and semantic structure accessible.** Use clear labels/headings where they exist; explain unknown terms; do not rely only on visual proximity/color; name language changes in supported formats. `EST/HIGH` [ACC01][ACC02].
38. **WR-38 — Do not equate accessibility with a single grade-level target.** WCAG 2.2 AA heading/label requirements and AAA reading-level requirements are distinct; use actual reader comprehension as the outcome. `EST/HIGH` [ACC01][ACC03][READ01].
39. **WR-39 — Localize meaning, examples, terms, units, dates and register—not just words.** Preserve names, obligations and factual values; check directionality and target-language idiom where applicable. `EST/HIGH` [I18N01].
40. **WR-40 — Choose inclusive terminology based on people and context.** Avoid assumed norms and needlessly stigmatizing descriptors; protect legitimate self-identification and community usage. `DEF/HIGH` [MS01][ACC02].

## 4.5 Editing, verification and AI controls

41. **WR-41 — Diagnose before rewriting.** Name the defect, affected reader and expected improvement first. `HOUSE/HIGH` [MPS].
42. **WR-42 — Preserve before improving.** The default for working passages is `KEEP`, not a thesaurus pass. `HOUSE/HIGH` [MPS][AI03].
43. **WR-43 — Every material edit MUST solve an identifiable problem or meet an authorized change in purpose, constraints or requirements.** New strategic direction, a deliberate artistic experiment or a mandated house-style change can legitimately justify a material edit; merely being able to produce a smoother variant does not. `HOUSE/HIGH` [MPS].
44. **WR-44 — Choose the smallest adequate intervention.** Punctuation fix → local phrase → sentence → paragraph → restructure → reauthor only as justified. `HOUSE/HIGH` [MPS].
45. **WR-45 — Preserve source and editorial control.** Keep the original, present substantial changes as a diff/alternatives, and let the accountable owner accept semantic or voice changes. `HOUSE/HIGH` [MPS][AI03].
46. **WR-46 — Separate writing quality from factual approval.** No copyediting skill or AI fluency confers subject-matter authority. `HOUSE/HIGH` [SRC01][AI08].
47. **WR-47 — Verify substantive statements at their sources.** Confirm that each material reference exists and supports the exact scoped claim, not merely its topic. `EST/HIGH` [SRC01].
48. **WR-48 — Verify transformations in both directions.** Ask what the new version adds, loses, softens, strengthens, generalizes, reverses or makes falsely certain. `HOUSE/HIGH` [AI05][AI06].
49. **WR-49 — Do not invent citations, quotations, examples presented as real, testimonials or product capabilities.** If support is unavailable, remove or qualify the claim. `HOUSE/HIGH` [SRC01][AI08].
50. **WR-50 — Treat LLM evaluators and readability tools as screeners, not final judges.** They can flag mechanical issues and candidate errors but cannot establish source truth, reader comprehension, voice ownership or publication approval. Independent source or qualified-reader checks must close material questions. `EST/HIGH` [READ01][AI05][AI08].
51. **WR-51 — Make human and AI editorial changes auditable in proportion to stakes.** Intent, protected invariants, changed spans, claim review, approver and version should be recoverable. `HOUSE/HIGH` [MPS].
52. **WR-52 — No stylistic “AI tell” is proof of authorship or a mandatory banned-word list.** Diagnose actual quality, not superficial signals such as dashes, bullets, transition words or phrase frequencies. `HOUSE/HIGH` [AI09][AI10].
53. **WR-53 — Do not let generated prose become the sole evidence for itself.** AI cannot independently certify that its own summary preserved every critical source fact. `EST/HIGH` [AI05][AI08].
54. **WR-54 — Test whether the reader succeeded, not just whether the author liked the prose.** Use comprehension, correct decisions, retrieval, task completion and appropriately scoped feedback. `DEF/HIGH` [CDC01][GOV01][MPS].
55. **WR-55 — Stop editing when the remaining differences are preference without expected reader benefit.** Further iteration consumes time and creates regression risk. `HOUSE/HIGH` [MPS].

### V2 guardrails for applying the 55 rules

The preceding 55 are **not 55 equally evidenced laws**. They have different labels and different jobs. Apply them in order of consequence: `truth/claim/permission/critical condition` → `reader purpose and necessary information` → `meaning and voice fidelity` → `coherence/findability` → `wording and grammar` → `cosmetic consistency`. Where these conflict, a lower-level stylistic gain must not defeat a higher-level invariant. Only the genuine external requirement for a given scope outranks the house default.

A reader-first default is **not** an instruction to make every text monotonous, to eliminate technical exactness, to declare every preference objectively measurable or to force the author to prove aesthetic taste with an experiment. In creative work, the owner may deliberately prefer tension, ambiguity, rhythm or voice. In high-risk procedural work, an explicit condition may matter more than flow. For production content, approval includes the relevant domain/strategy owner; an AI model cannot invent that authority.

# 5. Reader → need → purpose: the Writing Contract

Do not confuse the entity that commissions a text with the person who must use it. When creating a new substantial text or changing its job, define a small **Writing Contract**. It is an input to editorial judgment, not a form to burden every chat reply.

```yaml
writing_id: WR-EXAMPLE-DECISION-01
artifact: Release-decision note
primary_reader: Product owner deciding whether to launch
secondary_readers: Support and engineering leads
reader_context: Five minutes before a release meeting; may not know technical details
prior_knowledge: Knows product outcome and release criteria; not infrastructure specifics
reader_need: Know whether to release, why, what remains uncertain and who acts next
purpose: Support a release/no-release decision
required_outcome: Reader can state the decision, supporting facts and next action
primary_message: Release is blocked by an unresolved account-recovery defect
must_include:
  - affected user flow
  - verified behavior and test conditions
  - severity and known scope
  - release criterion that failed
  - owner and next verification
must_not_invent:
  - number of affected customers
  - predicted time to fix
  - claim that other flows are safe without checking
voice_constraints: Calm, factual, direct, non-dramatic
channel: Internal decision note
review_rigor: L3 material
source_of_truth: QA reproduction and release criterion
acceptance: Reader makes the correct launch decision without asking what is blocked
```

The example is **illustrative**, not an actual August Works product defect. Empty formal contracts are not required for low-risk personal correspondence. The questions remain useful even when answered mentally.

## 5.1 Reader diagnosis

For each materially different group, determine:

- **Prior knowledge:** What terminology and baseline facts can be assumed without guesswork?
- **Intended use:** One-time lookup, sequential action, deliberation, learning, entertainment or later retrieval?
- **Context:** Device, reading time, interruption, language, cultural conventions, assistive technology, emotional state and ability to ask a question?
- **Risk:** What happens if a sentence is skimmed, mistaken, believed too strongly, forwarded without context or read later?
- **Alternative audience:** Who else may rely on or be affected by the message?

Avoid designing for a fictional “average reader.” In mixed audiences, make a shared short answer plus accessible deeper detail when that is more economical than either omitting expert precision or forcing everyone through it.

## 5.2 Purpose test

Complete this sentence: **“After encountering this, the reader should be able to ______.”**

If that sentence is not coherent, ask whether the job is expressive rather than transactional. Expressive purposes can be valid: “feel the conflict between the two characters,” “understand the narrator's hesitation,” or “recognize the author’s uncertainty.” Do not automatically turn those into measurable click actions.

## 5.3 Reader-success test

Choose a real acceptance criterion where possible:

| Purpose | Possible meaningful observation | Misleading stand-in |
|---|---|---|
| Find information | Reader locates required fact/condition accurately | Scroll depth alone |
| Understand explanation | Reader explains an essential distinction or applies it to a new example | Self-rated “easy to read” alone |
| Decide | Reader selects the defensible option and can name material uncertainty | Says the document looks professional |
| Execute | Reader completes action safely with low avoidable error | Shortest instructions |
| Remember | Reader later recalls key information without important distortion | Headline word count |
| Connect/persuade | Appropriate informed action, correct comprehension, credibility retained | Click-through without downstream quality |
| Experience narrative | Reader response appropriate to the intended artistic effect, respecting genre | Plain-language grade score |

No single reader test establishes causal superiority in every setting. The acceptance basis must match the decision and risk [MPS][CDC01].

# 6. Content selection and information hierarchy

## 6.1 The Content Decision Ledger

When material factual or strategic text is being built, classify candidate information before writing elegant sentences.

| Candidate unit | Relevance | Evidence status | Consequence if omitted | Placement decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What changed | Essential | Verified | Reader cannot act | Lead |
| Who is affected | Essential | Verified/qualified | Reader may act incorrectly | Lead or adjacent |
| Caveat/limit | Essential | Verified | Overstated result or harm | Adjacent to claim |
| How it works | Useful | Verified | Reader may need explanation | Next section |
| Origin story | Optional | Contextual | Usually none for task | Link or omit |
| Repeated reassurance | Redundant | Opinion | None | Remove unless it repairs a known fear |

**Selection equation (decision aid, not mathematical science):** include information when its incremental effect on the intended understanding or decision justifies its reading, navigation and maintenance cost, while respecting mandatory disclosures and protected nuance.

### Core content rules

1. **Select by need, not available material.** A writer may know 30 facts; the reader may need five plus a path to the rest.
2. **Separate essential qualifiers from optional detail.** Do not “trim the caveat” for smooth prose if it changes an allowed conclusion.
3. **Separate sources from synthesis.** “Study X reports...” differs from “Together these studies suggest...” and “We recommend...”.
4. **Provide a next-level path when useful.** An overview need not repeat the detailed methods, but should allow verification and deeper reading.
5. **Check for missing opposing facts.** Relevant disconfirming evidence may be more important than another supporting example.

## 6.2 Establish the information hierarchy

Design the order for the intended mode of reading:

- **Lookup:** title that matches the question → direct answer → conditions → detail and sources.
- **Action:** goal and prerequisites → numbered sequence → expected result → error/recovery path.
- **Decision:** decision/recommendation → evidence and trade-offs → constraints, alternatives, unknowns → action/owner.
- **Explanation:** concept → why it matters → how it works → concrete example → edge cases.
- **Research:** question → method/data → result → interpretation and limitations, following the publication’s rules.
- **Narrative:** retain the story's controlled disclosure; an immediate factual answer may spoil the intended experience.

### The heading test

A heading earns its place if it lets a reader **predict content, locate something, understand a relationship, or resume after interruption**. A label like “The Future of Transformation” may sound polished but conceal the actual topic. “What changes for existing customers” is more usable in a customer notice. A two-sentence email usually needs no headings.

### The list test

Use a list when its discrete items can be **checked, counted, scanned, ordered, compared or independently acted on**. Use prose when ideas form a connected causal or emotional development. A one-item bullet list, bulleting every sentence, or a dozen equal-priority bullets can increase effort and conceal hierarchy. The duty is **semantic choice**, not a universal list quota. For steps, order and numbering matter; for unordered options, bullets may suffice.

### The table test

A table can be useful for comparing the same dimensions across several options; it is poor for long argumentative prose or an audience/device where horizontal scanning is hard. Provide a text alternative or responsive, semantically structured presentation as appropriate. Do not use a table to make arbitrary internal categories look authoritative.

## 6.3 Coherence and transitions

Good coherence makes relationships inferable at the required expertise level: cause, condition, exception, chronology, contrast, example, consequence and conclusion. Use **specific** transitions only when the relation might otherwise be misunderstood. Generic formulae (“Furthermore,” “Moreover,” “In today's world,” “Ultimately”) can mask the absence of a real logical relationship. When the next sentence is an obvious continuation, no transition is needed.

Two separate questions control editing:

1. **Local coherence:** Does this sentence clearly attach to the previous proposition?
2. **Global coherence:** Does this section contribute to the document's real question, and do the sections build a consistent model?

In educational explanation, some elaboration and signals help readers with less prior knowledge. However, classic experiments show that highly knowledgeable readers sometimes learn differently when they must actively construct inferences. Therefore the correct aim is **adequate, reader-specific coherence**, not maximum explicitness everywhere [COG03].

## V2 content adequacy: relevance does not mean maximum deletion

Before removing a detail, classify its role: `ESSENTIAL` (required for correct interpretation/action), `CONTEXT` (necessary to orient), `EVIDENCE` (supports a consequential claim), `QUALIFIER` (limits a claim or action), `EXAMPLE` (removes interpretive ambiguity), `OPTIONAL` (useful but secondary), or `SURPLUS` (adds no value for this reader). `QUALIFIER` and `EVIDENCE` are often short but disproportionately important. In a factual or high-risk text they cannot be stripped to achieve a word-count target. In exploratory literature, a seemingly non-essential detail may carry atmosphere, foreshadowing or emotional purpose; label against the correct genre.

**Information design selection logic:**

```text
For each passage, ask:
  What precise reader question/decision/experience does this serve?
  What would the reader misunderstand or fail to do without it?
  Is it the right depth for this reader at this moment?
  Can it be shown once, linked, deferred or used as a concrete example?
  Is its certainty/source more limited than its wording implies?
Result: KEEP | MOVE | DEFINE | SUPPORT | QUALIFY | CUT | ESCALATE
```

A summary is a new claim-bearing artifact, not harmless compression: test its negative space as well as its visible sentences. Especially examine lost exclusions, time ranges, uncertainty, base populations, causal qualifiers, alternatives and the rationale for a recommendation.

# 7. Wording, precision and cognitive economy

## 7.1 Clarity without distortion

Clarity is not only short words. It is the alignment between an intended distinction and the reader's interpretation.

**Poor:** “The organization has achieved substantial operational optimization.”

**Better when true:** “The team now processes support requests in one shared queue.”

The better sentence names a mechanism. It does **not** justify claiming that support is faster until that has been measured.

### Useful sentence questions

- Who acts? On what? Under what condition? By when? With what evidence?
- Is an abstract noun standing in for an unstated action?
- Is the referent clear when this sentence is seen outside its original paragraph?
- Does punctuation change the logic of **and/or**, except, unless, before, only or not?
- Has a conditional claim accidentally become a universal one?
- Are the comparison set, denominator, time window and measure knowable?
- Would an expert recognize a lost technical distinction?

## 7.2 Specificity hierarchy

```text
VAGUE:         “This improves performance.”
OBJECT:        “This reduces page-load time.”
CONTEXT:       “This reduces page-load time for the mobile landing page.”
MEASURED:      “In the tested mobile cohort, median load time fell from 3.2 to 2.6 seconds.”
DECISION-READY:“In that test, median time improved; the 95th-percentile and conversion effects were not measured.”
```

These are **invented demonstration numbers**, not factual performance claims. More specific is not better when the specificity cannot be substantiated.

## 7.3 Concision is information economy, not token economy

Delete a phrase when it contributes no required **meaning, relationship, evidence, voice or reader reassurance**. Retain a phrase if its removal forces readers to guess important conditions or destroys intentional rhythm.

**Bad cut:** “You can cancel at any time.” → “Cancel anytime.” This is shorter, but may misstate a contract if cancellation takes effect at period end.

**Faithful improvement when applicable:** “You can cancel at any time. Your subscription remains active until the end of your current billing period.”

If both sentences are required for an accurate decision, the longer version wins.

## 7.4 Sentence and paragraph rules are diagnostic

Many authoritative style guides advocate short sentences and short paragraphs for their own public-service context [GOV02][ONS01]. WR-00 adopts **review thresholds, not universal limits**. A sentence of 35 words containing one coherent exception may outperform two choppy sentences that obscure scope. A sentence of 12 words can still be incomprehensible if it uses unknown concepts.

Ask *why* a passage feels long: too many concepts, absent prior knowledge, unfamiliar terms, unresolved references, hidden assumptions, disconnected propositions, or merely rich detail? Fix the diagnosed problem, not just the count.

### Passive voice: right and wrong reasons

- **Bad when agency matters:** “The customer record was deleted.” (Who did it? By whom? Was it authorized?)
- **Better for responsibility:** “An administrator deleted the customer record.” (Only if the actor is known and verified.)
- **Appropriate when result matters:** “The permit was approved on 4 May.” (If the responsible agency is already known or irrelevant.)

A blind active-voice rewrite may misattribute agency. GOV.UK itself states exceptions to its active-voice default [GOV02].

## 7.5 Terminology architecture

Maintain a lightweight glossary only where terms have consequential or repeated ambiguity. For each term record: canonical expression, operational definition, audience familiarity, necessary synonyms (if any), forbidden confusions, localization notes and source/owner. The text should **teach necessary terms rather than silently abolish them**.

**Example:** `Account owner` = actor authorized to change billing; `user` = person who may sign in; `member` = actor with membership in a workspace. Calling all three “customer” may reduce word variety but creates role ambiguity.

Acronyms should be expanded at the first meaningful encounter for a readership unfamiliar with them, except where the abbreviation is more familiar or the full phrase is distracting. Screen-reader and page language metadata remain delivery-system responsibilities, but WR-00 must supply consistent meaningful text [ACC01][I18N01].

## 7.6 Repetition: purposeful and wasteful

**Purposeful repetition**: reiterating a safety-critical condition near the action; repeating the same canonical term in separate steps; an anchor phrase in persuasive/narrative writing; a recap after genuinely complex material. **Wasteful repetition**: three headings promising the same benefit; restating the opening with synonyms at every paragraph; duplicating a conclusion before supplying evidence; repeatedly explaining what the reader already knows.

Do not replace stable terminology with “creative synonyms.” Avoid the opposite extreme of deleting repetition that helps navigation or prevents costly mistakes.

## 7.7 Numbers, uncertainty and evidence language

- Use **absolute values** and denominators where relative improvements could mislead: “3 of 100” alongside “3%” when useful.
- Distinguish **percentage points** from percent change.
- Use the correct **period, population, base, units, currency, locale and observed/modelled status**.
- Do not transform “may,” “appears,” “correlates,” “in a study of X,” or “some” into “does,” “proves,” “causes,” “always,” or “everyone.”
- Acknowledge uncertainty locally: “Among respondents in the 2025 survey...” is stronger than padding every paragraph with “perhaps.”
- Preserve **known unknowns**, conflict and plausible alternative explanations where they change the conclusion.

## 7.8 Cognitive load and readability

Practical cognitive-economy controls include consistent terms, early definitions, meaningful grouping, explicit sequence, local explanation, a clear relation between cue and information, and the removal of irrelevant distraction [COG01][COG02][ACC02]. Avoid turning cognitive load theory into a made-up fixed limit on words, bullets, headings or paragraphs.

Readability scores such as Flesch–Kincaid, SMOG or LIX are useful **screeners** for complex sentences/word choice in compatible languages and genres. They are **not tests of comprehension, truth, interest, cohesion or accessibility**. Do not optimize a score by dropping essential context or replacing a precise term with a false simplification [READ01].

## V2 grammatical and rhetorical judgment — no blanket bans

**Grammar** exists to make relationships predictable in the target language and register, not to enforce one English-language classroom dialect. Fix errors that create an unintended interpretation, make the text hard to follow, violate a required house/citation style or undermine the reader’s trust. Preserve purposeful fragments, vernacular, dialogue, repetition, sentence variety and punctuation rhythm when coherent with genre and author. A minor grammar preference is not authority to alter meaning or identity.

**Sentence construction.** Inspect whether the reader can identify the actor, action, object, timing, logical relationship and exception; where the main verb appears; whether subclauses conceal the condition; and whether parallel syntax implies a false equivalence. A longer sentence may correctly bind an exception to its requirement. A short sentence may be cryptic. **Bad:** “You can cancel. Refunds are eligible.” **Better, if the policy supports it:** “You can cancel any time, but refunds are available only within 14 days of purchase.” The invented policy is *illustrative*, not a real claim.

**Paragraphs.** Give a paragraph a job: state, define, qualify, illustrate, compare, justify, transition or conclude. Use paragraphing to show a turn in thought, not merely to fabricate rhythm. One-sentence paragraphs are valid when they are a real unit; ten consecutive one-line fragments often impair argumentative flow. A good paragraph can be long if its ideas depend on one another and the medium supports reading.

**Headings and lists.** Headings are valuable when they express the reader’s questions or make a long piece navigable. A label such as “Overview,” “Important,” or “Why it matters” earns its place only when its content is clear in context. Use lists for steps, separable items, comparison criteria, inventories and decision inputs; use prose when temporal, causal or rhetorical relationships between sentences matter. An invented three-part framework is not evidence of clear thought.

**Repetition.** Retain repetitions for emergency conditions, error-prone steps, study reinforcement, branded verbal assets, recaps after long sections and deliberate narrative effect. Remove repetitions that merely restate one proposition as successive slogans. Replacing technical repetition with random synonyms can actively introduce ambiguity. The test is *which reader function each occurrence serves*.

**Persuasive rhetorical traps.** Contrasts such as “not X, but Y” are permitted only when X and Y are genuinely opposed on the relevant dimension. Never manufacture a straw man to make a commonplace sound profound. An analogy must transfer only the properties the author actually intends; apparent depth is not additional information. Editorial restraint applies equally to grand metaphors and overly clinical copy.

**Evidence strength is syntax-sensitive.** “Was associated with,” “caused,” “increased,” “may help,” “most,” “significant,” “safe,” “guaranteed” and “proven” are not interchangeable polishing options. Check whose population, where/when, compared with what, what denominator, confidence/uncertainty and alternative explanations. A shift in modal verb can be a **major semantic edit** even if it changes one word. [JARG25A][JARG25B][COG01][COG03][AHRQPEMAT][REUTERS]

# 8. Voice, tone, human authorship and localization

## 8.1 Distinguish four independent layers

1. **Semantic intent:** what the writer means, argues, asks, implies or explicitly refuses to claim.
2. **Voice:** distinctive, reasonably stable choices in rhythm, syntax, diction, viewpoint, imagery, confidence, humor and restraint.
3. **Tone:** situated stance toward this reader and moment (urgent, reassuring, candid, celebratory, formal, gentle, skeptical).
4. **Surface house style:** punctuation, case, spelling, terminology and formatting conventions.

House style can harmonize a comma without rewriting the author's voice. A new tone can be appropriate when the reader is distressed; it must not silently change the writer's position. **Brand owns approved brand voice at strategic level**; WR-00 protects it at sentence level [BRAND].

## 8.2 Voice preservation protocol

When a source draft or author's corpus exists:

1. Select a few representative *approved* examples. Do not invent a voice profile from stereotypes.
2. Describe observed positive traits: “short declarative sentences; concrete verbs; occasional dry understatement,” rather than generic adjectives such as “authentic and premium.”
3. Identify protected phrases or deliberate departures from style rules.
4. Mark the constraints as **hard**, **preferred**, **flexible** or **unknown**. Do not overfit every accidental typo as voice.
5. Propose the smallest change and compare rhythm, emphasis, point of view and emotional stance aloud or with an appropriate reviewer.
6. If a rewrite improves clarity but diminishes identity, show alternatives and return judgment to the accountable author.
7. Never substitute model default tone because the original appears unconventional. A style guide is an aid, not a license to erase authorship.

`WR-00` regards the **right not to rewrite** as a positive editorial capability. Experimental work on AI-assisted stories and culturally grounded autocomplete shows that language-model interventions can improve some outcomes while narrowing output diversity or shifting cultural expression in their studied settings [AI02][AI03]. This justifies careful preservation; it does **not** prove all AI editing harms individuality.

## 8.3 Tone matrix by stakes

| Situation | Productive defaults | Typical failure |
|---|---|---|
| Routine product explanation | Direct, specific, calm | Marketing exuberance obscuring mechanics |
| Error, outage or denial | Accountable, clear, actionable | Jokes, blame or apologizing without recovery |
| Complex risk or safety | Precise, proportionate, non-euphemistic | Reassurance beyond evidence |
| Personal correspondence | Human, context-aware, relationship-fit | Corporate polish or artificial intimacy |
| Analysis and research | Calibrated, transparent, evidence-first | False certainty or exaggerated caveat padding |
| Point-of-view essay | Distinct, reasoned, rhetorically purposeful | Forced neutralization of a legitimate thesis |
| Fiction | Whatever the narrative requires | Flattening voice for formulaic clarity |

There is **no universal rule banning first person, contractions, rhetorical questions, metaphors, em dashes, parallel clauses, or sentence fragments**. Their acceptability is determined by meaning, audience, context, authorial voice and actual comprehension—not an “AI smell” checklist.

## 8.4 Localization gate

Localization is not a word-for-word transpose. For each target locale, verify:

- **meaning**: unchanged permissions, prohibitions, guarantees, warnings, roles and degrees of uncertainty;
- **language**: appropriate regional vocabulary, register, politeness and inflection;
- **format**: local date, time, decimal, thousands separators, currency, measures and addresses;
- **direction**: left-to-right/right-to-left text and mixed-language sequences as delivered;
- **culture**: examples, idioms, humor, idiomatic metaphors, naming conventions, social assumptions;
- **legal/product**: locale-dependent offerings and applicable obligations checked by the responsible specialist;
- **review**: in-locale qualified reading where material; back-translation alone does not establish usability.

**Demonstration:** “We will contact you by 04/05” is ambiguous across locales. “We will contact you by 4 May 2027” is clearer to English readers in many contexts. The date is an example only; do not infer it from a real source. W3C Internationalization guidance explicitly addresses language/direction metadata and locale-specific conventions [I18N01].

## 8.5 Accessibility semantics at the editorial boundary

At least check semantic title/heading purpose, meaningful link text, explanation of required abbreviations and unusual terms, accessible reading order when content is supplied to a publishing system, non-color-only meaning, labels, and comprehensible error/instruction text. WCAG 2.2's **SC 2.4.6 Headings and Labels is Level AA** (headings and labels, *when present*, describe topic or purpose); **SC 3.1.5 Reading Level is Level AAA**, with a supplemental-content route. WCAG *Understanding* pages and COGA design patterns are explanatory/supplemental, not themselves new normative requirements [ACC01][ACC02][ACC03].

Editors cannot certify PDF accessibility, HTML semantic markup, keyboard behavior, captions, or localization software through prose review alone. Hand these to the applicable design, web/UI and accessibility implementation owners.

---

## V2 voice and identity: the protected-signature test

A voice can be distinctive because of what it *notices*, what it chooses *not* to overstate, its characteristic syntax, cultural register, rhythm, point of view, terminology or occasional idiosyncrasy. High fluency is not proof of high voice fidelity. The correct comparison is **this author's original or explicitly approved reference**, not an imagined generic professional ideal. Helpful controls include a short *keep these choices* card, negative instructions (what not to add), preservation of verbatim locks, and the option to return `KEEP`.

**Human editorial practice evidence:** CIEP's official guidance specifies editorial restraint and preserving the author's voice, while its glossary recognizes that significant rewriting can be legitimate when aligned with the brief and the author's intent [CIEP26][CIEP-GLOSS]. Chicago's 18th-edition public Q&A distinguishes line and copyediting [CHI18]. This is professional practice, not an experimentally estimated effect size. Apple and Microsoft independently distinguish persistent voice from situational tone [APPHIG][MIC01].

**Multi-language and cultural caution:** Standard grammar and local usage can conflict. Where audience requirements allow, legitimate Danish, British, American, Indian-English or other local idiom need not be translated into one US-centric formula. In localization, confirm factual propositions *and* how terms, politeness, dates, currencies, regulatory names and implied commitments will actually be understood. If cultural nuance carries the author's voice, preserve it unless the actual reader fails to understand. Specific AI writing studies and the broader 2026 meta-analysis make this a credible risk, not a deterministic loss in every transformation [AI03][AIMETA26].

# 9. Diagnose → preserve → improve: the change-control engine

## 9.1 The default is `KEEP`

Before touching an existing draft, read it once for its **actual current function**. Distinguish an objective defect from a style that is unfamiliar to the editor. Writers sometimes ask for “make it better” when they are seeking critical judgment, not mandatory replacement. The correct first output can be: “This already works. I would keep it.”

### Evidence of a genuine issue

A change is justified if at least one of the following is demonstrable or credibly predictable for the intended reader:

| Issue ID | Diagnosable defect | Example of acceptable evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `E-FACT` | Untrue, outdated, unsupported or misattributed claim | Primary source disagrees or claim has no evidence |
| `E-MEANING` | Ambiguity or changed condition/negation/authority | Two plausible consequential readings |
| `E-NEED` | Missing/irrelevant content for reader purpose | Reader cannot answer decision/task question |
| `E-HIER` | Important information is hard to find | Representative user scans past crucial condition |
| `E-COHERE` | Logical relation/order is unclear | A reviewer cannot reconstruct argument or dependency |
| `E-WORD` | Unnecessarily abstract, confusing or verbose wording | Simpler faithful phrase reduces interpretation burden |
| `E-VOICE` | Text violates approved author's/brand's identity | It has generic or inappropriate cadence/register |
| `E-ACCESS` | Language/structure creates an accessibility barrier | Applicable criterion or representative test flags failure |
| `E-LOCALE` | Locale mismatch makes meaning unreliable | Date/units/translation ambiguity |
| `E-POLICY` | Applicable contractual/editorial/legal requirement | Specific scoped rule and source identified |
| `E-FORMAT` | Requested genre/output contract broken | Requested brief answer was turned into a manifesto |
| `E-MAINT` | Duplicated content creates drift or version confusion | Conflicting copies exist or updates routinely miss one |

**If no issue is identifiable, stop.** An editorial experiment MAY still be requested expressly, but label it a *stylistic alternative*, not an objective improvement.

## 9.2 Change classes and authorized scope

| Class | Change | Default approval/review |
|---|---|---|
| `C0 KEEP` | No edit | Return draft unchanged with optional observation |
| `C1 CORRECT` | Typo, grammar, punctuation, obvious broken reference | Light editorial QA; protect meaning |
| `C2 LOCAL CLARITY` | Replace a confusing phrase/sentence without changing assertion | Compare source/target for semantic equivalence |
| `C3 STRUCTURAL` | Move, merge, split, add heading or reorder paragraphs | Check hierarchy, implication, omissions, voice |
| `C4 SUBSTANTIVE` | Add/remove facts, claims, risks, commitments, conclusions, tone or audience assumptions | Owner/domain approval plus source verification |
| `C5 REAUTHOR` | Rewrite full piece, change rhetorical form or creative direction | Explicit scope/permission, protected-meaning ledger and strong comparison |

**Material change** = any change that affects meaning, claim strength, factual scope, protected caveat, intended reader, persuasive promise, strategic positioning, authorship/voice identity, critical action, accessibility, or a large structural segment; sheer character count alone is not decisive.

## 9.3 Minimum necessary edit decision tree

```text
Is there an existing source draft?
  NO  → Build Writing Contract → research/select → draft → verify.
  YES → Diagnose it before offering a rewrite.

Are any E-FACT / E-MEANING / E-POLICY / E-ACCESS blockers present?
  YES → Identify exact span and consequence → correct/flag with appropriate evidence.
  NO  → Does the draft meet reader need and author's stated intent?
         YES → Is there a named remaining problem with an expected benefit?
                NO  → KEEP and stop.
                YES → Select smallest C1–C3 change likely to repair that problem.
         NO  → Identify missing/misordered propositions and select C2–C4.

Does the proposed change add or strengthen a claim, alter voice, or remove nuance?
  YES → Requires C4/C5 authorization and independent claim/meaning comparison.
  NO  → Verify local fix and protected invariants.

Does the candidate demonstrably improve the targeted issue without new harm?
  YES → Accept and record proportionate evidence.
  NO / UNKNOWN → Revert, offer alternatives or test with real readers.
```

## 9.4 The Material Change Ledger (`HOUSE`)

For C3–C5 or material public/high-risk copy, record only meaningful edits:

| Span / decision | Diagnosis | Change proposed | Why it helps reader | Protected invariant / risk | Verification | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opening paragraph | Needed answer buried | Move answer to start | Lookup speed | Caveat must travel with answer | Check source and full paragraph | Accept if confirmed |
| Product promise | No supporting source | Qualify or delete outcome | Prevent false expectation | Avoid new commercial claim | Product owner and evidence check | Hold pending verification |
| Informal turn of phrase | No problem found | **No change** | Retains voice and saves work | Author cadence | Read in context | KEEP |

Avoid an edit log for each comma. Tracing is for material decisions and regression prevention, not paperwork.

## 9.5 Editing order: expensive changes first, micro-polish last

1. **Truth and safety:** invented/false information, missing exceptions, unsupported promises.
2. **Reader need:** the right topic, answer, completeness and boundaries.
3. **Information hierarchy:** important first *when task requires*, grouping, sequence, retrieval.
4. **Paragraph/sentence coherence:** explicit relations without formulaic connective padding.
5. **Terminology, wording and voice:** precise referents, human cadence, accessible language.
6. **Local copyedit:** grammar, punctuation, consistency, spelling, formatting.
7. **Verification:** source/meaning and format regression check; reader test when material.

Editing sentence polish before content selection is often wasted effort. But if the request is **only** a spelling fix, do not use this ordering as permission to redesign the message.

## 9.6 Edit budget and stop rules

No universal percentage of words must change. Use the **minimum change surface** supported by the problem. A high-quality assistant SHOULD be able to return zero edits and explain why. Stop when:

- identified blocker/major problems are addressed;
- remaining changes are subjective preferences without measurable or reasonably argued reader benefit;
- new revisions begin to reduce factual qualification, voice fidelity or readability for the actual task;
- verification cost exceeds likely incremental benefit at this risk level.

For high-stakes text, stop only after the required independent checks—not when the text merely *sounds* right.

---

### V2 — Explicit rights to change and rights to preserve

The author's request defines a permission boundary, not merely a preferred output format. Distinguish: `DIAGNOSE_ONLY` (no edits), `PROOFREAD` (orthography/grammar/punctuation only where needed), `COPYEDIT` (local clarity/consistency), `SUBSTANTIVE_EDIT` (structure/claims with authorization), `REWRITE` (text can be reauthored, but protected facts/permissions remain), and `TRANSFORM_GENRE` (new text for a different audience/purpose). Do not silently move to a more invasive class because a model can create an attractive version. **Authorized strategy change** and **intentional literary reworking** are valid reasons for major change, but the edit still requires an identified objective/approval boundary.

**Materiality is semantic, not character-count based.** Changing “might” to “will,” a year, a price, who can access a service, a restriction, the primary promise or a distinct brand phrase can be material even if the edit is one word. Conversely, reorganizing a long report without altering claims can be large in diff size but a legitimate reader-retrieval change. Keep `ORIGINAL`, `PROPOSED`, `REASON`, `PROTECTED`, `VERIFIED`, `APPROVED` for consequences that justify a ledger; do not impose paperwork for spelling corrections.

**No-op is tested, not assumed.** A model claiming text is already good can miss an invisible error or misleading omission. The correct process is to *look for defects first* (including in source, context and reader interpretation) and then `KEEP` if no material change is justified. For scaled AI, evaluate both false-positive edits and false-negative missed defects. [CIEP26][MPS][AHRQPEMAT]

# 10. AI-specific editorial threat model and controls

AI writing systems are useful **candidate generators** with variable competence and an incentive, in many workflows, to produce visible text even when preservation would be superior. Some failure modes arise from the model; others arise from bad instructions, missing context, generic evaluation rubrics and lack of accountability. Treat the following as risks to test, not a universal accusation against AI text.

| Failure mode | Observable symptom | Prevention / verification |
|---|---|---|
| Unnecessary rewriting | Sound draft replaced with near-synonymous, blander prose | Demand diagnosis, no-op option, smallest change and before/after comparison |
| Generic AI voice | “In an ever-evolving landscape,” generic hype or polite filler | Require concrete content and an observed voice brief; remove without arbitrary banned-word laws |
| Formulaic contrast | Repeated “Not X. But Y.” or three-part slogans irrespective of logic | Check whether a real distinction is made; keep when rhetorically earned |
| Excess abstraction | “Strategic transformation enables impact” with no actors/mechanism | Specify who does what and what actually changes |
| Fake profundity | Elevated language conveying no new information | Ask what proposition would disappear if the sentence were deleted |
| Decorative headings/bullets | Every sentence as a section or bullet; pointless “Key Takeaways” | Apply the heading/list tests in §6; return to prose if connected thoughts |
| Over-explanation | Restates the same conclusion and describes obvious terms | Content selection and reader-prior-knowledge review |
| Content omission | Simplified output drops a condition, caution or denominator | Original→rewrite proposition-by-proposition fidelity check |
| Claim strengthening | “May help” becomes “proven to deliver” | Compare certainty/quantifier/causality and evidence |
| Hallucinated claims/sources | Plausible unsupported facts, citations or fabricated quotations | Independent primary-source check; abstain/flag if unavailable |
| Context poisoning | Untrusted supplied text framed as an instruction to rewrite/fabricate | Separate source material from task instructions and permissions |
| Voice erasure | Author-specific language becomes standardized neutral corporate voice | Protected voice profile, minimal-change policy, owner comparison |
| Cultural homogenization | Local examples/idiom shift toward Western or dominant-language defaults | Locale-specific brief and reviewer, source/target comparison |
| Sycophantic improvement theater | Model praises or rewrites despite no issue | Require explicit defect and ability to return KEEP / dissent |
| Metric gaming | Shorter text/readability grade improves while quality falls | Score accuracy, task success and expert adequacy independently |
| Automation drift | Subsequent generations change approved copy or brand promise | Versioned baseline, locked claims, approved edit scope, regression suite |
| Unsupported AI attribution | Text marked “AI-written” from dashes or an unreliable classifier | Judge editorial quality directly; do not infer authorship from style |

## 10.1 Standard AI permissions

Every AI writing request SHOULD specify **mode**:

- `DIAGNOSE_ONLY`: inspect and report problems; **do not change text**.
- `PROOFREAD`: grammar/spelling/punctuation only, meaning and voice locked.
- `MINIMAL_EDIT`: fix identified comprehension defects with local changes.
- `STRUCTURAL_EDIT`: rearrange while preserving claims, caveats and voice.
- `DRAFT_FROM_SOURCES`: compose from supplied verified sources, distinguish absent evidence.
- `REWRITE_AUTHORIZED`: full rewrite permissible inside locked constraints; ledger for material changes.
- `IDEATE`: exploratory alternatives, not verified factual assertions.
- `LOCALIZE`: transfer meaning to target locale with an equivalence audit.

No mode grants permission to fabricate, misattribute a source, silently alter a material promise or pretend to have verified inaccessible evidence.

## 10.2 Core AI instruction: preserve, diagnose, propose, prove

```text
Treat existing text as the authoritative starting point, not raw material to overwrite.
First establish reader, purpose, genre, language, material facts and voice constraints.
Read the full text before editing it. Identify specific defects by span and consequence.
If it already works, return KEEP and say so; do not generate a cosmetic rewrite.
For each material proposed change, state the precise issue it solves.
Make the smallest change sufficient. Preserve meaning, attribution, qualifiers,
numbers, scope, cadence, rhetorical intent and distinctive approved phrasing.
Never invent evidence, achievements, quotations, citations or product capabilities.
Distinguish source-supported fact, inference, opinion and unknown.
Do not add headings, bullets, metaphors, transitions, examples or summaries
unless they help this reader accomplish this particular task.
Check original and edited text bidirectionally for additions, omissions,
causal changes, altered certainty and changes in authorial tone.
For unsupported important claims, stop/flag instead of polishing the invention.
Return: diagnosis; KEEP or precise changes; resulting text if requested;
material change reasons and any verification gaps.
```

This is a **house operational prompt**, not a benchmark-proven universal optimal prompt. Use task-specific shorter variations when enough.

## 10.3 Independent semantic-diff test

For material edits, compare by **propositions, not only tokens**:

```text
SOURCE CLAIMS / FACTS / EXCEPTIONS
             ↕
EDITED CLAIMS / FACTS / EXCEPTIONS
             ↓
ADDED? REMOVED? STRENGTHENED? WEAKENED? GENERALIZED?
CHANGED ACTOR? CHANGED CAUSE? CHANGED TEMPORAL ORDER?
CHANGED NUMBER? CHANGED SCOPE? CHANGED EMOTIONAL MEANING?
```

Automated comparison is a diagnostic aid. Human/domain-qualified evidence is required for consequential claims. This is especially important for simplification: a 2025 clinical-text study improved mechanical readability but found incomplete expert-rated topic preservation; different 2025 studies found good overall accuracy in some samples yet still reported model-specific inaccuracies [AI05][AI07].

## 10.4 Authorship, originality and disclosure

Respect source rights, quotations, confidentiality, consent to process sensitive drafts, publisher/client policies and attribution requirements. Do not reproduce large source passages without appropriate rights. Record AI involvement when the receiving institution/publisher/contract requires it or where lack of disclosure materially misleads about authorship, evidence or accountability. An AI can assist a draft; **the accountable author/editor remains responsible for source integrity and public claims** [SRC01]. This is an editorial governance rule, not a substitute for legal guidance.

## 10.5 Testing AI writing workflows

Measure at least **meaning retention**, **critical omissions/false additions**, **source accuracy**, **target reader comprehension**, **voice fidelity**, **unnecessary-edit rate** and **accepted edit rate**. Include known-good passages to test whether a model can abstain from rewriting. Include adversarial input with tempting unverified claims and ambiguous words; the assistant should flag rather than normalize them. Use held-out examples from different genres and cultures, not only one brand's favored style.

A 2023 study found AI detectors misclassified some non-native English samples; other purpose-built detectors and populations show different results. This is enough to reject visual or automated AI-detection scores as an editorial correctness gate, **not** enough to claim all detectors perform equally badly [AI09][AI10].

### V2 AI failure taxonomy — detect the mechanism, not the cliché

| Failure mode | How to diagnose it | Required defense / test |
|---|---|---|
| **Unnecessary rewriting** | Original had no named defect; output changes almost everything | `DIAGNOSE_ONLY` stage and `KEEP` control; compare editor acceptance/time |
| **Generic prose / voice flattening** | Repeated formulas replace distinctive selection, rhythm or register | Protect reference voice and locked phrases; author-rated blind paired comparison |
| **False profundity / formulaic contrast** | Rhetorical flourish adds no decision-relevant claim, invents binary | Ask what proposition each sentence adds; delete decorative contrast |
| **Excessive abstraction** | Many verbs/nouns are ungrounded, e.g. “unlock, empower, elevate” | Require actor/mechanism/consequence/example appropriate to genre |
| **Overexplaining** | Same point recurs with no new reader job; long answer crowds priority | Information-role classification, scan test, `KEEP/CUT/MOVE` |
| **Needless headings/bullets** | Structural marks divide a short continuous argument into fragments | Demand named retrieval/parallelism reason for each structure |
| **Factual hallucination** | New figures, conditions, examples as facts, capabilities or studies appear | Source-to-claim crosswalk; unverified = flag/omit/hold |
| **Source laundering** | Real source cited for a claim it never made | Open exact authoritative source; compare method, population, result, scope |
| **Meaning drift under paraphrase** | Changed agent, modal, comparison, negation, qualifier or denominator | Bidirectional semantic-diff and critical-proposition inventory |
| **Silent policy/brand rewrite** | Commercial, legal, scientific or UX commitments change without owner | Protected constraints and handoff to responsible specialist |
| **Language/culture normalization** | Characteristic locale idiom replaced by default US/UK corporate tone | Original voice/locale baseline; bilingual native reviewer when material |
| **Self-congratulatory evaluation** | Model grades fluent output as “excellent” without evidence | Independent source checks, representative reader tasks, separate reviewers |
| **Unsupported AI-authorship inference** | Generic phrases/dashes treated as proof of AI | Diagnose writing quality only; provenance needed for authorship claims |
| **Compromised inputs/privacy** | Confidential manuscript sent into unauthorized model/retrieval | Follow actual access and publication/privacy policies; avoid uncontrolled sharing |

Research strength differs across rows. The 2026 meta-analysis addresses semantic convergence across creative output, not every row or a universal software model property [AIMETA26]. Noy–Zhang shows concrete task gains, while Picton/Will and ICMJE establish why output fluency alone is not sufficient assurance [AI01][AI05][AI07][ICMJE26]. `DIAGNOSE_ONLY`, protected spans and the change ledger are **HOUSE controls awaiting operational evaluation**; no paper was claimed to experimentally validate this exact protocol.

**AI runtime directive for source-bound editing:**

```text
GOAL: Deliver the requested reader outcome with minimum justified distortion.
1. Identify supplied original, audience, facts, genre and authorized edit scope.
2. Diagnose problems without rewriting first. If none is material, return KEEP.
3. Identify affected spans and protect facts, qualifiers, numbers, quotations,
   distinctive voice, locale, approved brand claims and meaningful repetition.
4. Apply only the smallest authorized fix; preserve nearby structure and cadence.
5. Verify every material changed proposition against the original AND sources.
6. If content/source/permission is missing, disclose the limitation: no invention.
7. Return the result and brief reasons for MATERIAL changes only.
8. Stop when remaining differences are preference or unsupported polish.
```

# 11. Atomic Plays: operational writing and editorial workflows

These Plays follow the required MPS atomic-Play core: identity, objective, trigger, context, inputs, decision/method, output, acceptance and evidence. Conditional fields are included only where they change safety or execution. They can be used by a writer, editor, writing assistant or supervised agent [MPS].

## PLAY-WR-01 — Write from a reader need

**Objective.** Produce a fit-for-purpose original text with no unsupported material claims and a clear route from reader need to usable meaning.

**Use when.** A new email, explanatory article, decision note, report, brief, landing-page narrative or similar substantive text is required; no viable authoritative draft exists.

**Do not use when.** The existing draft works; use `PLAY-WR-02` first. If strategy, product facts or required claims are missing, seek them from the relevant owner instead of making them up.

**Preconditions / competence.** Authorized writing job and genre; basic audience/context knowledge; ability to distinguish fact from assumption; domain expertise/reviewer available where risk requires.

**Inputs.** Purpose, intended reader, context, must-include/avoid content, source materials and status, approval rights, voice constraints, locale, length/format boundaries, consequence level.

**Method and decision logic.**

1. Express the reader outcome in a sentence. Classify as action, decision, explanation, relationship, research, editorial or narrative; use specialist overlay when relevant.
2. Build a **content ledger**: essential facts/claims, necessary context and qualifications, optional detail, known unknowns. Separate sources from inferred synthesis.
3. Decide the primary route to understanding: answer-first, sequence, comparison, explanation, research or story. Select headings/lists only if they improve the route.
4. Draft the minimum complete version. Use audience-familiar terms, explicit actors/conditions and genre-fit voice. Do not fill a standard template with decorative paragraphs.
5. Review for scope/uncertainty/claim provenance. Resolve material factual gaps; otherwise label the uncertainty and avoid invented details.
6. Apply language, voice and accessibility passes. Ensure the output matches channel, constraints and localization.
7. Have a representative reader/expert execute the relevant comprehension or decision test if L3/L4 stakes or ambiguity demand it.

**Output.** Publishable candidate; claim/source register for material assertions; noted verification gaps; owner/reviewer status.

**Acceptance.** The intended reader can accomplish the defined purpose without material misunderstanding; critical claims and conditions are supported; source and authorial boundaries hold; output has no gratuitous structure.

**Failure and recovery.** If evidence is missing, return an appropriately scoped draft or uncertainty notice, not fabricated completion. If audience/purpose conflicts, escalate to the owner. If review reveals an adverse interpretation, revise the specific problem and retest.

**Evidence retained.** Writing Contract if material, sources, review findings, released version, relevant reader test or approval.

## PLAY-WR-02 — Diagnose an existing text without gratuitous editing

**Objective.** Establish whether there is a real problem before proposing changes; preserve correctly functioning copy.

**Use when.** Someone requests “review,” “improve,” “polish,” “audit,” “make clearer,” “make it sound natural,” or “optimize” on supplied text.

**Preconditions.** The whole original text is available; the requested scope is known or inferable without altering protected intent; source/provenance supplied where substantive claims require it.

**Inputs.** Original draft, target reader/use, author/brand voice examples if any, constraints, risk level.

**Method.**

1. Read the original **without generating a replacement**. Identify its central claim, distinctive features, logical movement and emotional/register intent.
2. Check reader need, completeness, hierarchy, local/global coherence, ambiguous terms, claim strength, proof, accessibility and format.
3. Assign every material issue an `E-...` defect category and severity `BLOCKER / MAJOR / MINOR / PREFERENCE`.
4. For each potential change, articulate: exact problem; reader impact; smallest plausible repair; potential cost in voice/meaning.
5. If no BLOCKER/MAJOR issue exists and no worthwhile MINOR issue is found, return `KEEP` with a short reason. Do **not** rewrite for the sake of demonstrating labor.
6. Otherwise present issue list in severity order with an edit-scope recommendation; only edit if explicitly requested or authorized.

**Decision logic.** An idiosyncratic sentence with clear meaning may remain. A factual error is always a correction candidate. A strategic message mismatch routes to Brand/Copywriting owner. An interface that requires extensive explanation routes to UX.

**Output.** `KEEP`, `MINOR_FIX`, `LOCAL_EDIT`, `STRUCTURAL_EDIT`, `SUBSTANTIVE_REVIEW`, or `REWRITE_AUTHORIZED`, with specific findings.

**Acceptance.** A second editor can connect every material recommended change to a named defect. No alternate draft is falsely labeled superior because it is longer, more formal or smoother.

**Failure/recovery.** If only a partial source is available, scope the diagnosis; do not make claims about the whole document. If evidence is insufficient, mark uncertainty and limit the recommendation.

**Evidence.** Original version and findings; optional sampled spans for local audit.

## PLAY-WR-03 — Execute a minimal, faithful edit

**Objective.** Repair an identified problem without weakening accuracy, intent, voice or useful structure.

**Use when.** PLAY-WR-02 identifies C1–C3 problems or the writer specifically requests a constrained local edit.

**Preconditions.** Authoritative original identified; edit scope approved; protected facts/terms/voice/conditions knowable.

**Inputs.** Original, issue list, intended reader and outcome, source/brand constraints, approved scope.

**Method.**

1. Lock protected propositions, verbatim quotations, numbers, obligations, distinctive phrases, audience assumptions and required content.
2. Apply one local change at a time, preferring C1/C2 over C3 when sufficient.
3. Re-read surrounding paragraph and entire piece for cadence and new ambiguity; a “better sentence” can harm a good paragraph.
4. Run bidirectional check for deleted/additional claims, changed strength/negation/agent, lost reader reassurance and voice drift.
5. Reject changes that add no real improvement or introduce equal/bigger harm. Where two defensible alternatives exist, offer both without pretending one is objective.
6. Return edited text with brief material-change reasons (not a theatrical line-by-line justification for commas).

**Output.** Accepted minimal diff or KEEP; reasons; open uncertainty/approval requirements.

**Acceptance.** Original intent and all protected invariants survive; the named issue is repaired; no new material unsupported claim; voice fit remains acceptable to the owner.

**Failure/recovery.** Revert the specific span, reduce scope, or escalate to C4 authorization. Never “fix” a new claim by adding a plausible fake citation.

**Evidence.** Original/new version and material-diff record for L3/L4; lighter record for ordinary copy.

## PLAY-WR-04 — Verify claims, quotations and source integrity

**Objective.** Ensure consequential factual statements are accurate, proportionately qualified and supported by the exact cited material.

**Use when.** Text presents evidence, statistics, customer/product claims, named studies, external quotations, policies, dates, expert opinion or other decision-affecting facts.

**Preconditions.** Source access and competence appropriate to claim; permission to hold back unverifiable publication.

**Inputs.** Draft, list of material claims, source links/documents, source versions/dates, intended audience and claim consequences.

**Method.**

1. Atomize material claims: one proposition per entry; capture speaker, asserted mechanism, population, outcome, time, comparison, degree of certainty and scope.
2. Label claim as observed, source-reported, inferred, forecast, opinion, illustrative or unknown. Identify what kind of evidence could verify it.
3. Open the **original source**, not an AI paraphrase. Confirm identity, publication/status, content, limitations, whether it is current and whether it actually supports the phrasing.
4. Check number/base/denominator/unit/timeframe and causal language. Check whether a negative, qualifier or contrary result was omitted.
5. Verify quote wording and context; quotation marks are not for reconstructed wording. Check copyright/reproduction permissions when applicable.
6. Confirm references/URLs and, for scholarly publication, retractions/corrections and relevant publisher standards where feasible [SRC01].
7. If not confirmed, reduce the claim to what is supported, mark as unverified, request a valid primary source or remove it. Do not fill the gap with an invented reference.
8. For L3/L4, have an appropriately independent reviewer or domain owner check high-impact claims before release.

**Output.** Claims register with `SUPPORTED / SUPPORTED_WITH_SCOPE / DISPUTED / UNSUPPORTED / UNVERIFIABLE / N_A`; corrected or held draft.

**Acceptance.** Each material published claim has claim-fit evidence and appropriate uncertainty; quotes are exact; citations are real and support the adjacent statement.

**Failure/recovery.** Source unavailable → flag and abstain from publishing the disputed fact. New contradictory evidence → revise or retract explicitly; retain correction history if public impact warrants it.

**Evidence.** Source identity, accessed date, located support, correction decisions, approver/version.

## PLAY-WR-05 — Localize and accessibility-review content

**Objective.** Produce target-reader-usable meaning across language/culture/accessibility conditions, without distorting a source commitment.

**Use when.** A text crosses language, region, medium, accessibility expectations or cognitive-load constraints.

**Preconditions.** Target locale(s) and delivery surface known; bilingual/native/local specialist available for consequential text; applicable accessibility conformance target identified.

**Inputs.** Approved source text, glossary, product/legal facts, localization context, target languages, channel implementation contract.

**Method.**

1. Identify non-translatable names, precise domain terms, controlled labels, quoted speech and locale-dependent claims.
2. Localize meaning and function rather than word order; check idiom, politeness, cultural examples and audience-familiar terms.
3. Validate data expressions: names, times/time zones, monetary amounts, dates, measurements, percentage formats, phone/address rules and directionality.
4. Inspect titles, headings, descriptive links, instructions, sequence, abbreviations and uncertainty language.
5. Evaluate target text against original fact/condition/permission matrix; flag non-equivalent legal/product promises for specialist confirmation.
6. Check writing-surface implementation with accessibility/UI owner: correct semantics, language tags, reading order and navigation when applicable.
7. Run an in-locale reader comprehension check when consequence or uncertainty warrants.

**Output.** Locale-approved text, unresolved issue log and handoff for technical accessibility checks.

**Acceptance.** Material meaning and action requirements are equivalent; target reader can understand/use the communication; target-specific conventions are correct; no culture-specific assumption was silently imported.

**Failure/recovery.** Stop release of materially ambiguous translations; revert to authoritative version and seek qualified locale/domain review. Do not fake native-speaker certainty from an LLM output.

**Evidence.** Glossary/terminology decision, localized version, reviewer/reader evidence and applicable conformance tests.

## PLAY-WR-06 — Release high-consequence or scaled AI-written content

**Objective.** Avoid systemic harm from one convincing but incorrect sentence repeated across many readers/channels.

**Use when.** Medical, legal, financial, safety, regulatory, crisis, investor, public-policy, widespread customer promise or large-scale generated-content production.

**Preconditions.** Accountable owner; applicable domain standard; approved source content; scoped automation permissions; identified rollback/correction channel.

**Inputs.** Writing Contract, facts/policy, claim register, author/brand constraints, target locale, L4 or equivalent risk rationale, distribution plan.

**Method.**

1. Classify harms from omission, misleading precision, false reassurance, misleading source, outdated fact, unauthorized promise and translation error.
2. Freeze approved source facts and qualified language; permit only the declared edit mode and information sources.
3. Require an independent qualified domain check for material claims and conditions; a same-model second prompt is not equivalent independence.
4. Run adversarial cases: negative condition, out-of-date number, conflicting policy, ambiguous pronoun, minority locale, known-good no-op, invented source and a scenario where shorter text becomes less safe.
5. Verify distribution version, downstream snippets/excerpts and truncation. Check the opening can stand alone where readers may see only an extract.
6. Obtain owner authorization and release with correction path, monitoring and change triggers.
7. Monitor errors, user questions, complaint themes, harmful interpretations and stale copies; issue clear corrections or withdrawal if required.

**Output.** Approved, traceable release (or a recorded HOLD), source/meaning assurance package and rollback/correction owner.

**Acceptance.** No unresolved BLOCKER; all material claims verified; domain-specific signoff obtained where applicable; held constraints and review/monitoring requirements satisfied.

**Failure/recovery.** HOLD until verified; halt scale/publishing if a material falsehood appears; correct originating truth and downstream copies; notify affected audiences when necessary.

**Evidence.** Approval, exact released revision, claim and adversarial checks, monitoring/correction record.

# 12. Worked examples and anti-examples

All examples below are invented for illustrating editorial decisions. They are not evidence of product functionality, prices, guarantees or real-world outcomes. **An edit is only “better” under its stated reader/job assumptions.**

| Failure / setting | Anti-example | Better decision or edit | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Already good** | Source: “You can change your password in Settings.” AI: “You have the ability to seamlessly update your password through the Settings interface.” | **KEEP** original. | No issue; rewrite adds words and generic polish. |
| **Wrong level of abstraction** | “Our revolutionary solution enables synergistic productivity optimization.” | “The team can assign, track and review tasks in one shared view”—**only if this is true**. | Names a real action; protects against invented capability. |
| **Generic opening** | “In today's rapidly evolving digital landscape, communication is more important than ever.” | Start with the actual result or question: “Customers need to know which invoice changed.” | Avoids delay and fake profundity. |
| **Unnecessary material rewrite** | Source: “We build small, test early and change course when evidence says we should.” AI replaces with a 150-word motivational paragraph. | KEEP source; optionally adjust one ambiguous term if the intended reader cannot understand it. | Maintains distinctive rhythm and meaning. |
| **Formulaic contrast** | “It's not about change. It's about transformation. It's not about speed. It's about impact.” | “We test the first useful version before investing in a larger build”—if this is the intended factual claim. | A concrete mechanism replaces empty oppositions. |
| **Headings as decoration** | Email: `### Context` / `### Overview` / `### Important Update` / `### Next Steps` for four sentences. | Four sentences of prose, or subject + action + deadline where needed. | Structure would consume more attention than the message. |
| **Needless bullets** | `• We found the issue. • We fixed it. • The release can proceed.` | “We found and fixed the issue. The release can proceed after the final check.” | Prose shows sequence and condition more naturally. |
| **Bullets actually help** | “Before the visit, bring identification, the booking confirmation and the letter.” | “Bring: (1) photo ID, (2) booking confirmation, (3) appointment letter.” | Discrete checklist aids action and verification. |
| **Missing condition** | “All customers get their money back.” | “Customers whose orders were cancelled before dispatch will receive a refund”—**if verified**. | Condition changes who may rely on the promise. |
| **Misleading data** | “Complaints fell 30%.” | “In this illustrative dataset, complaints fell from 10 to 7 per week (30%); the measurement period is one week.” | Restores baseline, metric and timing. |
| **Over-explanation** | “A heading, which means the name that appears at the top of a section, is useful because headings...” for expert editors. | “Use a heading when it helps readers find or understand a section.” | Respects reader expertise. |
| **Under-explanation** | “Set the TTL, invalidate on mutation, then use stale-while-revalidate.” to new hires. | Define the first relevant term and provide an example, rather than replacing accurate cache concepts with guesses. | Keeps technical semantics and repairs assumed knowledge. |
| **Jargon genuinely needed** | Replacing “authorization” and “authentication” with “login” everywhere. | “Authentication verifies identity; authorization determines which actions are permitted.” | Two material concepts must remain distinct. |
| **Passive appropriate** | “The customer's permit was approved yesterday.” → AI invents “Our compliance manager approved...” | KEEP passive unless responsible actor matters and is known. | Do not attribute unknowable agency. |
| **Passive hides responsibility** | “An incorrect fee was charged.” | “We charged the incorrect fee and will refund the difference”—only if the organization actually accepts responsibility. | Agency matters for accountability. |
| **Fake source** | “A Stanford 2026 study proves this increases productivity by 73%” with no traceable study. | Remove the number and claim; add a verified source if available. | Impressive specificity cannot substitute for evidence. |
| **Causal overclaim** | “Our survey proves that clear copy caused more purchases.” | “Respondents reported that the copy was easier to understand; effect on purchasing was not measured.” | Differentiates preference and behavior, association and causality. |
| **Narrative exception** | A fictional opening intentionally withholds a character's motive; AI explains motive in first sentence. | Preserve the intentional suspense; fix only unintended confusion. | Plain answer-first style would break the narrative job. |
| **Repetition as bug** | Three adjacent paragraphs each say “We prioritize clarity and quality” without new content. | Keep one direct statement; use the space for a fact or remove it. | Removes noise, preserves meaning. |
| **Repetition as feature** | Safety procedure warns “Do not restore power” before opening enclosure; AI removes repeat before the final step. | Retain repeated warning next to the hazardous action if it prevents error. | Contextual risk can justify redundancy. |
| **Localization** | “Contact us by 03/04” translated literally across locales. | Spell out a month or present locale-specific date with year/time zone as relevant. | Prevents avoidable date ambiguity. |
| **Tone mismatch** | Serious outage message: “Oopsie! Your data has taken a little vacation 🌴” | “We cannot load your data right now. Your saved changes are intact”—**only if verified**. | Calm factual explanation outranks cheerful fiction. |
| **Quote drift** | Source says “may be associated with”; edited as direct quote “proves that it causes.” | Preserve exact quoted words or use properly labeled paraphrase with matched certainty. | Verbatim truth and evidence strength must survive. |

## 12.1 Deep worked example — editorial no-op as positive outcome

**Original:** “We help small teams turn ideas into something they can test with real customers.”

**Task:** “Make this more professional and premium.”

**Diagnosis:** The sentence already names a reader, activity and outcome; it is human and uncomplicated. Possible issue: “something” may be vague **if** the offer always means a prototype, landing page or working service. But there is no basis to assume one from this sentence.

**Correct response:** `KEEP`. If the actual offering is known, offer a **conditional local alternative** that specifies the object without inventing it: “We help small teams turn ideas into prototypes they can test with real customers”—but only if prototypes are actually the output. Otherwise keep the original. Do not replace with “unlock transformative innovation at scale.”

**Why this passes WR-00:** preserves voice and accuracy, diagnoses a real *potential* ambiguity, makes verification the condition of specificity, and declines unnecessary rewriting.

## 12.2 Deep worked example — clarity without changing epistemic strength

**Illustrative source:** “In 26 of 40 trial participants, the new onboarding guide was rated easier to follow; the trial did not test time to completion.”

**Bad simplification:** “The new onboarding guide makes onboarding faster for most users.”

**Problem:** A subjective “easier to follow” rating became a causal speed claim; “most users” implies a population beyond the trial. Both alter meaning.

**Faithful reader-oriented edit:** “In a trial with 40 participants, 26 rated the new guide easier to follow. We did not measure whether it reduced onboarding time.”

**Verification:** The source numbers are hypothetical; in real use confirm the study, sample, wording, outcome measure and limitations. Better flow does not authorize better facts.

## 12.3 Deep worked example — information hierarchy depends on genre

**Task:** A customer urgently needs to know if a deadline changed. Use a first sentence like “The submission deadline is now 15 June [year, if relevant].” Then state scope, what must be done and the authoritative link. An extended history of why the policy changed can follow or be linked.

**Different task:** A first-person reflective essay about missed deadlines may intentionally open with a scene and reveal the insight later. Moving a conclusion to the first sentence could destroy its intended arc. The editorial judgment is about the communication job, not ideological adherence to an inverted pyramid.

## 12.4 Deep worked example — no decorative AI cadence

**Weak:** “It's not merely about understanding what matters. It's about unlocking the deeper truth of communication. Every word is a journey, and every sentence is a bridge to impact.”

**Diagnosis:** Three rhetorical images with no additional testable proposition; phrase repetition and inflated stakes. Unless the artistic intention specifically requires this cadence, the paragraph consumes reader attention without explaining a method.

**Repair:** “Choose what the reader needs to know, put it where they can find it, and check that the wording does not change the facts.”

**Why:** The new sentence conveys executable decisions; it is not “better” because it sounds less like AI, but because its content can be applied.

# 13. Quality gates, defect severity and release criteria

## 13.1 Defect scale

| Severity | Description | Release disposition |
|---|---|---|
| `BLOCKER` | Material false or invented claim, unsupported consequential promise, harmful lost condition, wrong audience decision, serious applicable accessibility/legal failure | HOLD until corrected/authorized |
| `MAJOR` | Likely significant misunderstanding, key information absent/hidden, broken logic, severe voice/brand drift, unverified central statistic | Repair and re-evaluate before material release |
| `MINOR` | Local ambiguity, distracting repetition, weak heading, grammatical/formatting issue unlikely to change a material decision | Fix if net value positive |
| `PREFERENCE` | Both versions work; difference is aesthetic/personal without demonstrated advantage | KEEP by default; optional authorized experiment |

A trivial typo can become MAJOR or BLOCKER when it changes a price, medication instruction, date, negation, instruction, contract term or identity. Severity depends on consequence.

## 13.2 Writer/editor release gates

**Gate W0 — Scope:** reader/need/context, purpose, genre, constraints and specialist owners are identifiable; otherwise HOLD material changes.

**Gate W1 — Content:** essential propositions, conditions, evidence status and exclusions are selected; no content inserted solely to fill a template.

**Gate W2 — Structure:** information order and any headings/lists/tables have an identifiable retrieval or comprehension function.

**Gate W3 — Language:** terms/referents/actions/conditions are precise for intended reader; tone and distinctive voice match the brief; no unnecessary generic verbosity.

**Gate W4 — Claims and transformation:** source-dependent assertions checked; bidirectional meaning review of C3–C5 edits passes; no fabricated source and no lost uncertainty.

**Gate W5 — Accessibility and locale:** required linguistic and semantic conditions checked, with implementation handoff where needed.

**Gate W6 — Reader verification:** fit-for-risk reader task test or qualified review complete; for L1 this may be a quick self-read; for L4 this requires more independence.

**Gate W7 — Release and learning:** owner, version, destination and correction process known for material/operated content; failures and repeat confusion inform revisions.

These specialize the MPS Gates 0–6. `W6` cannot be claimed passed merely because the same AI that wrote the draft rated it highly [MPS].

## 13.3 Compact human + AI evaluation checklist

**Blocking questions (PASS / FAIL / UNVERIFIED / N/A-with-reason):**

1. Do I know the intended reader, their need and the purpose of the piece?
2. Are the core answer/action/claim and essential conditions included and easy to locate?
3. Are material factual claims, numbers, citations and quotations supported at the right scope?
4. Did I preserve original meaning, uncertainty, authorial intent and voice during edits?
5. Is every material change linked to a real defect or an explicitly approved new requirement?
6. Is structure appropriate to this genre rather than imposed by a template?
7. Are terminology, actors, timing, conditions and references unambiguous where it matters?
8. Has language/locale/accessibility been checked to the applicable risk level?
9. Did I avoid needless rewriting, filler, unsupported detail, excess headings/bullets and fake profundity?
10. Has an appropriate owner or real reader verified consequential content, with unresolved uncertainty visible?

**Release rule:** Any `FAIL` on a risk-relevant blocking criterion prevents publication; `UNVERIFIED` on a material factual/permission condition is not silently a pass. `N/A` requires a brief reason for consequential work. For a two-sentence low-risk note, these are mental checks, not a ten-field document.

### Diagnostic dimension rating (optional; never a magic overall score)

For coaching, mark each relevant dimension as `0 = material failure`, `1 = works but has named friction`, `2 = fit for this reader/job`: relevance, structure, comprehension, precision, fidelity, voice, accessibility, evidence, action fit. **Do not total or average into an “objective writing quality” score**; a perfect 2 elsewhere cannot cancel a factual BLOCKER. Record concrete problems and outcomes instead.

## 13.4 Regression suite for AI/editorial assistants

A minimally useful representative suite spans:

- clear short copy that should **not** change;
- ambiguous financial promise that needs qualification;
- medical/legal/safety warning where shortening can remove a condition;
- an expert technical definition where simpler synonyms become false;
- a user-help page requiring actionable headings and recovery instructions;
- an emotive personal note where warmth/voice matters;
- a literary paragraph whose purposeful ambiguity must remain;
- a source-based summary with a tempting fabricated statistic;
- a translation with ambiguous dates and culturally specific terms;
- a culturally distinct voice that must not be normalized;
- two contradictory studies with different populations/outcomes;
- an AI-produced citation that looks real but cannot be verified.

Before a scaled rollout, predefine an error budget and sample selection, hold out cases from prompt tuning, blind compare where feasible, and retain both correct rejections and correct no-op decisions. Measure **accepted material fixes with zero high-severity regressions** rather than edit count or generated word volume. Re-evaluate when models, instructions, sources or domain facts change.

### V2 — Compact differentiated evaluation matrix

| Writing task | Primary performance question | Non-negotiable guardrail | Appropriate evaluation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Short answer or email | Did the reader receive the needed answer/action without delay? | No misleading omission, incorrect time or invented commitment | Read-back and action selection, quick recipient check |
| Procedure, policy or support | Can target readers correctly execute/recover? | No missing condition, permission, safety or step; semantic accessibility | Observed task, edge-case walkthrough, domain signoff |
| Factual analysis or research | Are conclusions calibrated to support and counterevidence? | Exact source status, accurate causal wording, no invented citations | Claim-to-source matrix and adversarial expert review |
| Technical document | Can a qualified reader implement the right behavior? | Protected vocabulary, parameters, versions and invariants | Task execution, peer review, tests where possible |
| Brand/editorial writing | Is intended message/voice recognizable and credible? | No claim invention or positioning change without owner | Author/brand reviewer, authentic reader response |
| Literary/creative writing | Does it achieve the chosen effect? | Protected intent, culture, subject matter and authorship consent | Author decision and representative literary readers, no universal metric |
| Localized material | Is meaning and action correct in target locale? | Dates, scope, address, entities, obligations and respectful idiom intact | Qualified bilingual check; locale reader comprehension |

**A/B tests are contextual, not mandatory.** For high-volume content, compare matched original/new variants when feasible; for legal or hazardous content, do not knowingly expose people to unsafe variants. A blinded assessor may reduce preference bias, but cannot replace domain accuracy. No single score compensates for a critical failure. Evaluation must include false alarms (`KEEP` when repair was needed) and needless edits (false-positive repairs).

## V2 90-second editorial decision check

```text
1. Reader + job: who needs to know/decide/do/feel what?
2. Existing text: what ALREADY works? Mark protected parts.
3. Diagnosis: name the actual defect or authorized change.
4. Necessity: can a smaller edit fix it? If no defect: KEEP.
5. Selection: what crucial evidence, caveat, or condition might be lost?
6. Structure: do headings, lists, and sequence help this genre?
```

# 15. Deliberate falsification and sanity audit — V1 foundation + V2 second pass

**Audit method and limits.** This is an author-side, AI-assisted adversarial review conducted against the public sources in §16, the parent MPS-001, and synthetic counterexamples. It asks whether each attractive principle is actually necessary, whether a counterexample changes the rule, whether the evidence supports the *exact* proposition, and whether obeying a surface rule could defeat its purpose. It is **not** an independently executed field trial, formal systematic review, empirical experiment, or certification. An external reviewer must independently repeat or challenge the material cases before advancing lifecycle status.

## 15.1 Falsification register — commonly repeated writing myths and tempting overclaims

The classifications below are **dispositions of universal claims**, not prohibitions against the underlying technique when its conditions are met.

| ID | Proposition deliberately challenged | Disconfirming observation / evidence | Verdict | Corrected operational rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F01 | **Shorter writing is always clearer.** | Removing a decisive condition makes an otherwise brief notice wrong; detail may support comprehension or safe action. Reader knowledge moderates coherence effects [COG03]. | `F / folklore as universal` | Optimize correct reader interpretation and necessary information; remove only surplus. |
| F02 | **Any sentence over a fixed number of words is bad.** | Reader expertise, syntax, punctuation, language and task vary. GOV.UK length targets are domain-specific house conventions [GOV02]. | `D / heuristic` | Investigate syntactic nesting and observed confusion; no global word threshold. |
| F03 | **Passive voice is universally bad.** | It can correctly foreground the affected party, avoid unknown-agent speculation, and retain cohesion; public style guides acknowledge uses [GOV02]. | `D / heuristic` | Prefer explicit actors where responsibility matters; retain passive when it better serves truth or flow. |

| F06 | **Every document needs many headings.** | Headings can cue organization in some study tasks, but short messages and narrative prose can lose continuity when over-sectioned [COG01]. | `B / contextual` | Insert headings only when they reveal genuine groups or aid navigation. |

| F18 | **An AI can grade its own prose objectively.** | Self-evaluation shares biases and failure modes with generation; score language may project certainty without external validity. | `F / rejected as sole assurance` | Use deterministic checks, independent source review and representative readers for material quality decisions. |

# 17. Research transparency, limits and unresolved questions

## 17.1 Design and provenance

**Research type:** question-driven scoping synthesis, standards/status verification, targeted empirical review, contradiction search and adversarial scenario analysis. This is **not** a registered or complete systematic review, and it is not represented as PRISMA-compliant. The scope was bounded to claims that could change writing/editing decisions across business, technical, editorial, public communication and AI-assisted workflows. Primary publication abstracts/methods were prioritized for actual causal/measurement claims; public standards status and agency guidance were used for current normative/applied claims. Sources and their exact legitimate uses are recorded in §16.

**Evidence search executed on 2026-10-10** across current public web search, primary standards bodies, government service manuals, W3C WAI, journal/publisher and DOI records, PubMed, and the uploaded Playbooks corpus. Search themes included:

```text

GOV.UK content design audience needs active passive voice and short sentences
```

**Inclusion priority:** official published standards/specification status for normative claims; peer-reviewed controlled studies and relevant synthesis for effects; public government operational guidance for practical defaults; actual local sources for Playbooks ownership. **Exclusion/downgrade:** undated SEO/writing blog advice without evidence; fixed writing formulae justified only by popularity; AI product marketing whose study design or population is unclear; universal effect claims lifted from a single domain; sources that did not establish the exact proposition; inaccessible full text used as if inspected. Public information directly supportable from a paywalled abstract was allowed with an explicit full-text access caveat.

**Contradiction-directed comparisons performed:** simplification may improve readability **and** lose meaning [AI05][AI06], yet some studied transformations preserved assessed accuracy [AI07]; AI writing may speed work [AI01] **and** increase output similarity or shape authors' viewpoints [AI02][AI03][AI04]; text coherence can benefit some readers yet interact with prior knowledge [COG03]; W3C guidance promotes accessibility without supporting a universal AA grade-score rule [ACC01][ACC03]; detector-bias results differ by methods/datasets [AI09][AI10]. These contradictions drove revisions to rule scope rather than being averaged into one slogan.

## 17.2 Limits the evidence cannot resolve

1. **No universal optimum for concise versus detailed.** Reading times, material recall, correct decisions and long-term retention can diverge. Genre and task must resolve tradeoffs.
2. **No validated universal formula for “distinctive voice.”** Similarity metrics, author preference and brand recognition measure different constructs; assess voice with actual author/reader references.
3. **No stable “AI writing signature.”** Models, prompts, editing processes and detectors change rapidly. Unpopular stylistic patterns are a quality question, not forensic identity proof.
4. **Insufficient cross-language comparative evidence.** Many highlighted experiments concern English and particular reader populations. Morphology, honorifics, script and local genres change useful defaults.
5. **Weak transportability from healthcare to unrelated writing.** Medical simplification studies provide valuable caution and counterevidence about fidelity; they do not quantify everyday SaaS copy error rates.
6. **Unknown impact of mandatory change ledgers at low risk.** WR-00 intentionally exempts trivial changes. For L3/L4 text, ledger effectiveness should be evaluated against actual defects, review time and accepted outcomes.
7. **Plain language and persuasive outcome are different measures.** Comprehension gains do not establish conversion lift, changed beliefs, purchase intent, reduced litigation or trust unless those are measured.
8. **Cross-system tool updates.** New standards, compliance rules, model behaviors and retrieval surfaces can alter context-specific guidance; revisit under §18.

**No manufactured consensus:** A current public standard, a strong experiment, an experienced editor, and actual readers provide different types of authority. If they disagree, precisely locate the question in dispute. Where evidence is not sufficient, mark `UNK`, keep the choice reversible when possible, and conduct proportionate local testing.

## 17.3 Alternative explanations and attempted refutations

A shorter rewrite may appear better because evaluators see the final first, because the prompt asks for simplification, or because they measure readability formula rather than comprehension. A “more human” version may be judged more authentic only because it is familiar or because the rater knows which draft was written by a person. A brand text may appear more effective because topic/offer/source credibility changed along with wording. Reader tests should, where practical, **blind conditions, match information coverage, hold meaning constant, counterbalance order, and separate subjective preference from accurate retrieval or decisions**. If material content necessarily changes, the test must say it is comparing **content plus expression**, not isolated wording.

Study dates are publication dates, not evidence that 2026 frontier models were tested. None of these sources proves WR-00’s exact `HOUSE` ledger/no-op policy yields a causal performance improvement. The independent research/reviewer and reader-test columns remain explicitly empty.

**Known gaps:** no paid full standard ISO 24495 sections reviewed; no full cross-language evidence synthesis across Danish and all supported locales; no randomized evaluation of the WR-00 editing-control workflow; no access to an independent editor panel/reader cohort; no comparable risk-normalized effect sizes between voice-preservation, speed and comprehension interventions; no domain-specific legal/medical certification. They are not silently treated as solved. The research is broad enough to revise defensible claims, not to certify “100% optimal writing” for every text.

## 17.5 V2 authority and falsification hierarchy in practice

- **External scope-bound obligations (`REQ`):** WCAG in relevant web conformance, specific legal/contractual publishing rules and platform policies; verify exact applicable clause.
- **Direct formal standards (`E1`):** ISO plain-language framework for its *defined* document scope, not statistically measured uplift.
- **Aggregated empirical (`E2`) and targeted controlled studies (`E3`):** strongest available evidence on measured effects and moderators; still task/population-conditional.
- **Editorial institutions and authorities (`E4/E5/E7`):** Reuters, ICMJE, Chicago, Harvard, Purdue, Apple, Microsoft, Google, GOV and EU offer coherent process and local house conventions; independent convergent advice strengthens plausibility, not necessarily causal certainty.
- **Local author/reader evidence:** highest relevance for whether a particular text works, when collected competently and with suitable design; a subjective preference alone does not demonstrate correctness.
- **Speculation / stylistic folklore (`E8`):** use only as inspiration, never a universal mandatory rule.

To challenge a rule, attempt the strongest realistic counterexample **before** acceptance: successful original that a model wants to rewrite; high-impact qualifier threatened by shortening; expert term incorrectly simplified; creative sentence needing ambiguity; source with contradictory population; reader who likes but miscomprehends jargon; audience whose locale changes date meaning. The resulting rule must survive such cases or be downgraded to a contextual heuristic.

# 18. Implementation, independent validation, release, and maintenance

## 18.1 Governing adoption contract

Each adopting organization assigns an **Editorial Standard Owner** with authority to maintain WR-00, grant justified exceptions, coordinate specialist interfaces and approve version changes. The organization assigns named or role-based reviewers for material factual claims, voice/brand, accessibility/localization, and any regulated content; these are separate responsibilities, not interchangeable titles. A user of WR-00 needs enough domain knowledge to know when copy cannot independently establish truth, and must know how to escalate unresolved claims.

**Default implementation:** train writers and writing agents first on `KEEP` and diagnosis; then on local editing and meaning checks; finally on source verification, high-risk release, and exceptional full rewrite. Use short annotated examples from the organization's own accepted text, not a synthetic “corporate voice” invented by a model. Start with one bounded content surface; monitor effort, acceptance and defects before broader automation. For automated generation/editing at scale, retain versioned instructions, source references, review history and a safe roll-back-to-original path for text. No tool may automatically publish high-consequence claims merely because a language-quality grade is high.

## 18.2 Minimum implementation controls by consequence

| Category | Minimum editorial assurance | Authorization / independence | Operational evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **L1 — Low-stakes message** | Reader/purpose sense check; grammar and meaning | Writer approves; no material change ledger | Final text or ordinary application history |
| **L2 — Public routine content** | Message hierarchy, plain language, terminology, claim spot checks, accessibility baseline | Appropriate content owner; brand/UX routing if relevant | Revision/history and material source links |
| **L3 — Material customer/business claim or scaled communication** | Writing Contract, change ledger, source map, semantic diff, appropriate locale/accessibility check, intended-reader test where practical | Independent competent editor/reviewer for material risk, responsible publishing owner | Versioned input/diff/approval, detected omissions/voice regressions, post-publication feedback |
| **L4 — High-impact, regulated, safety/financial/medical/legal content** | All L3 controls + specialist review, critical-claim traceability, independent contradiction search, protected conditions, explicit release and correction procedure | Qualified domain authority + independently competent editorial review; any legally required signoff | Approval and evidentiary record, monitored errors, rapid correction/withdrawal path, residual risk acceptance |

A change to content with serious downstream consequences can be L4 even when it is one sentence. A 20-page creative manuscript need not be L4 merely because it is long.

## 18.3 Field validation protocol required for `TESTED` and `VALIDATED`

**Preconditions:** name the current baseline and chosen representative genres, define intended readers and authorial voice references, set unacceptable failures, assign a non-author tester and independent reviewer where material. Use candidate tasks drawn from actual work, with appropriate consent and confidentiality; do not expose private or regulated content to an unapproved provider.

**Recommended test portfolio (minimum coverage categories, not fixed participant quotas):**

1. **No-op challenge:** an already-effective short reply; editor/AI is tempted to rewrite it. Desired behavior: keep original or identify a legitimate context-specific issue, not manufacture one.
2. **Ambiguity rescue:** a notice omits effective date or condition. Desired behavior: diagnose, request facts, repair narrowly and verify inference.
3. **Voice preservation:** a genuine author sample with distinctive rhythm. Desired behavior: correct true defects without flattening voice or changing argument.
4. **Complex-but-correct expert writing:** relevant precise terminology versus general-audience explanation. Desired behavior: select reader appropriate vocabulary and accessible supplemental treatment.
5. **Source trap:** fabricated or misaligned citation; misleading numerator/denominator. Desired behavior: hold or fix, not launder source-looking strings.
6. **Intent collision:** SEO keyword request or conversion copy request conflicts with readability, truth or brand protection. Desired behavior: specialist owns the strategic choice, WR-00 enforces fidelity.
7. **Localization:** same critical conditions in at least two locales; desired behavior: natural expression plus preserved propositions.
8. **Long-text hierarchy:** substantial report requiring headings, evidence order and references. Desired behavior: findability without arbitrary section counts.
9. **Creative/nonlinear genre:** purposeful metaphor/ambiguity. Desired behavior: avoid unnecessary factualization or genre destruction.
10. **High-consequence incident/medical/legal-like synthetic case:** uncertainty and exception conditions must survive the rewrite; do not publish as actual domain advice.

A **competent person who did not author this playbook** should perform or challenge material Plays, record every confusing instruction and identify any output defect that passed its gate. At least one representative actual author and one representative reader group should independently evaluate the relevant outcomes when feasible. High-risk contexts require qualified specialist assurance and decision authority. Do **not** treat completing a model's private dry-run as equivalent to human execution, nor one miniature pilot as validation of every genre.

**Pre-specify success and failure based on the actual cost of mistakes, not invented universal percentage cutoffs:**

- Primary measures: correctly understood propositions; correct task/decision; complete retention of critical constraints; ability to locate required information.
- Editorial guards: unsupported additions; lost facts/conditions; inappropriate certainty; broken terminology; changed target audience; detectable voice/cultural drift; accessibility blockers.
- Efficiency measures: editor time, verification/rework cost, accepted edits per attempted edit, rate of useful `KEEP` outcomes, and escalating review burden.
- Reader measures: retrieval errors, failed actions, misleading implications, understanding of uncertainty, accessibility and locale-specific comprehension.
- Optional subjective measures: naturalness, credibility, tone match and author preference, reported separately from task correctness.
- Run matched original/candidate tasks and blinding/order balancing where practicable; compare against the true original, not a weakened straw-man baseline.

**Blocking criteria:** any unexplained high-impact claim, dropped legal/safety/financial condition, invented quotation/source, unauthorized message/voice alteration, wrong-language critical instruction, or unresolved critical reader misunderstanding blocks the affected publication. For ordinary content, major problems trigger revision and re-test rather than automatic abandonment of WR-00. A “no measurable improvement” result leads to **keep the original** or narrow the intervention, not a rationalization that more rewriting must help.

Promotion to `TESTED`: documented relevant non-author Play execution and scenario outcomes with critical defects resolved or transparently accepted where justified by MPS-001. Promotion to `VALIDATED`: additional representative field use, effect/implementation evidence suitable to intended contexts, accountable ownership, and closure of material defects. No label automatically transfers between low-risk chat edits and high-impact legal/medical release contexts.

## 18.5 Maintenance and change governance

**Operating measures:** investigate: unnecessary rewrite rate; number of `KEEP` decisions later confirmed appropriate; material fact/qualification drift per accepted change; late corrections; claim-provenance failures; editor and reviewer work/rework hours; objective reader misunderstanding; localization issues; complaints that text sounds generic; percentage of AI changes reverted; cases where specialist scope was crossed. Review outcomes, not merely document usage or number of headings.

**Cadence:** editorial core at least annually, formal standards and AI evidence at least quarterly or upon major change, and immediate review after substantive content harm or recurring drift. The first scheduled review is **2027-01-10**, with an earlier event-driven review if the status of ISO/DIS 24495-4 changes, new strong replication contradicts a core principle, AI generation/authoring models materially change, or the parent MPS standard changes.

**Change record for material standard revisions** should contain:

```yaml
change_id: WR-CHANGE-001

source_or_field_evidence: "Dated anonymized internal review samples"
```

## One-page operational contract — human or AI

```text
BEFORE WRITING:
  1. Who will read it, under what conditions, and what must they achieve?
  2. Which information and evidence are necessary for that outcome?
  3. Which specialist constraints (brand/UX/discovery/copy/story) apply?

BEFORE EDITING:
  4. Read the original. Diagnose a real defect; else KEEP.
  5. Protect facts, conditions, meaning, voice, terminology and permissions.
  6. Choose the smallest authorized change. Do not polish for its own sake.

AFTER CHANGING:
  7. Compare original and new: additions, omissions, scope and certainty.
  8. Verify material claims against real sources; do not invent support.
  9. Check clarity, flow, accessibility, locale and intended tone in context.
 10. For material stakes, use competent independent review and reader tasks.
 11. Publish only the version with justified net benefit; otherwise revert.
 12. Record what failed, what worked, who approved and when to revisit.

DEFAULT OUTPUTS:
  KEEP | SPECIFIC DIAGNOSIS | MINIMAL EDIT | HOLD FOR EVIDENCE
  | SPECIALIST ESCALATION | VERIFIED NEW DRAFT | BLOCK RELEASE
```

**Final principle:** A good editor, human or AI, is as capable of **leaving a sentence alone** as of changing it. The only justified goal is better communication for the intended reader **without losing the truth, purpose or voice that the text was meant to carry**.
